## D244-ESTATE-ROUTINE-COMPETENCE-001 — Routine professional competence and proportionate uncertainty

DM procedure clarification prompted by the player's Day244 OOC concern that all estates face too many business checks or excessively high DCs. This enforces D199-ESTATE-AUTONOMY-001 and D225-ESTATE-REINVEST-001 prospectively from the unchanged Day244 early-evening checkpoint; it is not a claim that the player specified new numeric DCs or authorized a historical financial retcon. Applies to Blackthorn, Miregate, Reedbed, Old Distributary Fort and future registered estates.

- **Establish facts before choosing dice.** Qualified staff with suitable tools, materials, funds and sufficient time complete ordinary work competently. Routine production, measured inspection/counting, standard milling, ordinary maintenance and rework, established-route arrangements, conforming delivery/acceptance, agreed collections and due payments need no ability check unless a specific material uncertainty applies. Preserve actual work time, finite capacity, agreed terms, independent counterparties and separate economic legs. A readiness result can be a documented professional finding without a d20.
- **Do not multiply failure gates.** Use one check for one meaningful uncertainty. Do not independently roll negotiation, quality, dispatch, arrival, acceptance, payment and retention merely because the journal records those steps separately. Additional rolls need genuinely independent risks identified before drawing. Do not add a second random refusal to ordinary fulfillment of accepted terms, or an unexplained credit failure to an otherwise routine payment. New demand, new commercial assent or genuinely doubtful credit may still be uncertain. Existing demand windows/tables and settled results stand; no guaranteed custom or future orders.
- **Calibrate to the actual task.** Do not default every professional job to DC12–14 and +4. First ask whether failure remains plausible after normal time, equipment and skilled assistance; otherwise resolve without a roll. When a roll is needed, state the particular difficulty, relevant qualification or documented modifier, assistance, stakes and reason the outcome matters. Label estimated bonuses honestly. Do not simply raise all NPC bonuses or lower every DC to compensate for too many rolls.
- **Resolve repeatable work through time and resources.** If ordinary competent diagnosis/adjustment can solve a problem and the only uncertainty is effort, use a supported work scope and finite time/material cost rather than repeated pass/fail permission to do the job. Seek a real prospective quote or inspect current conditions where inputs are missing; do not invent historical invoices, prices or defects. A genuinely dangerous, novel or technically constrained test may still require a check.
- **Bound failures and keep work moving.** A failed check applies only to its declared stakes. Where supported, use a bounded delay, extra work or reduced output rather than indefinite closure. Do not invent a manufacturer specification, outside authority, weekly retry or missing paperwork solely from a low die. If a real technical dependency exists, identify the measured/observed issue, responsible expert, corrective action and earliest feasible opportunity. An old failed check with no recorded defect calls for a present professional assessment; it is not evidence of a particular historic defect. Repeated attempts need changed conditions/time, never another real-world invocation.
- **Preserve history and accounting.** All saved dice, accepted outcomes, balances, invoices, stock, ownership and current safety limits remain as recorded. This clarification does not reroll, reverse losses, accept rejected grain, commission the Fort, qualify upper-reach barges, invent a punt bill or advance campaign time. Existing open work receives the procedure below at its next eligible action; any eventual correction or new outcome needs its own evidenced journal legs.

Application to existing open work:

Stock/grade/time checks and conforming repeat fulfillment are ordinary professional work; do not add blanket sales or collection failure gates. Keep actual raw-log availability, drying time and route-specific freight requirements.
- Miregate: the Day244 rejected pilot remains 1,800lb at 26.80gp cost. Meret/Isbet obtain the buyer's actual specification and inspect the lot routinely, then price feasible rework or alternative disposal; no second generic rejection/diagnosis roll without a specific uncertainty.
