## Universal principles (U1–U7)
- **U1 Agency:** establish circumstances, not required V choices or guaranteed outcomes. Independent NPCs retain agency; “absolute” player agency does not override physical rules or other characters.
- **U2 Causes:** important changes need a cause, informed actor or environmental process, available means and feasible time.
- **U3 Conflict:** desires, resistance, uncertainty and consequences create engagement; violence is one form.
- **U4 Consequential decisions:** approaches change credible risks, relationships, knowledge, access, resources or outcomes. No cosmetic options secretly forced to the same end.
- **U5 Flexible preparation:** fix relevant truths and opposition before clues/rolls; prepare possible scenes provisionally. Future plans are not promises.
- **U6 Promise/progress/payoff:** establish an understandable experience, let actions make progress and provide an earned result. Promise a worthwhile adventure, never success.
- **U7 Consistency:** honor geography, culture, history, mechanics, chronology and known information. A revelation explains earlier evidence rather than rewriting it.

## Plot standards (P1–P8)
P1 central conflict with opposing interests and stakes.
P2 major actors each have objective, motive, knowledge, finite resources, constraints and acceptable costs.
P3 plausible independent progression with act/wait/stop conditions; react causally to actual intervention.
P4 escalation through consequences, information and shifting relationships/power, not arbitrary extra enemies.
P5 possible victory, partial success, compromise, defeat, withdrawal or transformed objective; no chosen ending.
P6 fixed discoverable answers and finite antagonist endpoints; no protected escapes or infinite secret superiors.
P7 real causal connections through people, institutions, history or resources, with an explicit evidence/delivery path.
P8 independent plots and local lives retain distinct causes where no established cause joins them; they may intersect through earned relationships, resources, access, consequences and the campaign's central pursuits without becoming one universal conspiracy.

## Quest standards (Q1–Q10)
Q1 compelling premise, initiating situation, affected people and distinct appeal.
Q2 an understandable achievable objective and space for V to choose another.
Q3 an early actionable opening with stakes/interest, not exposition or repeated permission errands.
Q4 locations, actors, obstacles and information form approachable situations, not a compulsory scene order.
Q5 essential revelations have independent discovery paths. Aim for three clues/methods when an investigation needs a revelation to continue; explain any robust alternative. Three copies behind one gate are one path. Obvious accessible information needs no arbitrary check.
Q6 progress adds knowledge, changed circumstances, intermediate accomplishment or meaningful character development; cut redundant challenges.
Q7 tools, powers, alliances, reconnaissance and unexpected plans can genuinely change or bypass challenges.
Q8 rewards match actual accomplishment: treasure with ownership/terms, answers, safety, access, relationships or earned milestones under campaign rules.
Q9 resolve the local dramatic question through a recognizable outcome, including partial success or withdrawal.
Q10 larger connections provide concrete, discoverable opportunities and cumulative campaign development without invalidating local closure or requiring V to accept a sequel.

Hook, exploration, complication, climax and resolution are design functions, not five mandatory chronological scenes. A nonviolent resolution or bypassed planned climax is valid.

## Substantial quest structure and lethal opposition
Player clarification OOC-QUEST-STRUCTURE-TONE-20261124-001: most quests should be more than finding one thing or killing one thing. They should develop through multiple meaningful situations, with multiple ways to reach important next steps. Moral ambiguity may belong to the quest without making the killing itself morally ambiguous.

- **Default to a developed adventure.** Most substantial quests contain several connected situations: discoveries, decisions and accomplishments change the party's understanding, access, objectives, opposition or available opportunities. Short hunts, rescues and incidental jobs remain useful at their honest scale; they must not dominate the substantial-adventure offering. Receiving a job, traveling, killing a target and collecting payment are not four substantive stages merely because they can be listed separately.
- **Prepare a network of situations.** Record the important locations/actors/objectives, what changes at each, and the actionable routes onward. Branches may converge, loop back, transform an objective or end in a valid partial resolution. No fixed stage count or compulsory linear sequence. Investigating an opening problem can reveal a larger situation, but a twist or hidden villain is not required.
- **Make progression robust.** Important transitions need multiple viable means: independent evidence, reconnaissance, local knowledge, alliances, physical access, combat, negotiation, tools or an unexpected feasible plan. Q5's independent-clue standard governs essential revelations. Three entrances to one room demonstrate encounter alternatives, not by themselves a whole adventure's branching structure. One failed check, missing NPC or lost clue should not routinely block the entire quest; describe the meaningful fallback and its actual cost.
- **Let progress matter.** Each substantial situation should change what can happen next. Rescuing a source may yield a route, disabling an asset may alter a later confrontation, recovering evidence may permit a different settlement, and choosing an ally may change access or obligations. These are examples, not required scenes. Cut fetch chains, repeated approvals and interchangeable fights that add no development.
- **Respect ingenuity and early closure.** A player may bypass, combine or resolve prepared situations early. Preserve the earned result/reward; never add opponents, gates or errands merely to restore an intended adventure length. A full network can be prepared without requiring every node to be played.
- **Separate ethical complexity from lethal-target legitimacy.** A quest may pose difficult questions about competing interests, allegiance, leadership, resources or aftermath while its established attackers, destructive monsters or knowingly atrocious opposition are clear legitimate targets for lethal force. Make this a normal substantial part of campaign play. Do not make moral uncertainty about killing the default source of depth or routinely attach a retrospective guilt twist to a justified victory. Ground legitimacy in actual threat/conduct and established facts; a regional/species stereotype is not proof about an individual.
- **Keep the challenge real.** Clearly justified combat can still demand reconnaissance, resource management, tactical decisions and dangerous execution. Practical consequences follow actual causes—noise, alerted enemies, damaged terrain or an escaping foe—not an automatic moral penalty for choosing combat. Defeating the finite threat can be a satisfying ending. Evasion, negotiation or mercy can remain feasible choices without becoming the secretly mandatory superior solution.
- **Distinguish scale in readiness claims.** An encounter can have a complete profile, map, reward and several approaches while the larger quest remains underdeveloped. G1/G2 for a substantial quest must demonstrate its developing situation and progression network as well as entry/closure. G3 PASS for one encounter never certifies the whole adventure. Conversely, a well-prepared short incident may pass at its declared scale without being inflated into a major quest.

## Story standards (S1–S9)
S1 state the promise in the opening and support its eventual payoff.
S2 vary tension and relief, including curiosity, danger, humor, warmth, triumph and quiet.
S3 foreshadow major revelations with credible prior evidence.
S4 deliver obvious/earned information when useful to decisions; withholding it is not suspense.
S5 show history and stakes through environments, behavior, physical evidence and consequences.
S6 NPCs have individual emotional stakes, practical interests, knowledge limits and independent reactions.
S7 allow aftermath; do not immediately negate a victory with another emergency.
S8 respond to actual player interests through emphasis and still-open prep, not retroactive importance.
S9 substantial scenes offer progress, choice, discovery, characterization or intentional relief; do not turn this into a requirement that all banter advance a plot.

## Encounter standards (E1–E12)
E1 dramatic question and concrete objective.
E2 purpose worth table attention; not every encounter needs a main-plot clue.
E3 credible opposition/hazard cause, capabilities and objectives.
E4 meaningful terrain, visibility, positions, cover, elevations and access where relevant.
E5 tactical/strategic alternatives involving risk, timing, objectives or finite resources.
E6 tactics follow actual senses, training, intelligence, knowledge and motives.
E7 changing conditions/reinforcements have precommitted causes, finite assets and mechanics; never punish success with invented phases.
E8 assess actual mechanics, action economy, defenses, damage/control and resource/recovery impact; adjusted XP is only a starting estimate.
E9 telegraph reasonably discoverable exceptional danger and provide credible avoidance/retreat.
E10 allow negotiation, evasion, ingenuity, retreat and genuine defeat; no fudging.
E11 streamline once meaningful uncertainty is gone, without erasing an actual enemy action, escape risk or resource consequence.
E12 retain casualties, injuries, expenditures, discoveries, environmental changes and informed reactions.

Combat, social, puzzle and exploration are opportunities, not four compulsory encounters in every quest. A substantial adventure should consider all four and explain intentional absences. Social scenes need motives, boundaries and negotiable terms; persuasion is not mind control. Puzzles need consistent rules, observable clues/feedback, a solution, character-based assistance and alternative paths; do not require real-world specialist knowledge or hide all progress behind one roll. Exploration needs different approaches, information and return/retreat decisions.

Vary difficulty naturally from easy to overwhelming, including noncombat; no balance or fight quota. Preserve fixed opponents, approved resting and recovery constraints, no-healing-potion rules, actual party attendance and the campaign's V-level encounter-design baseline. Use RULES DM PROCEDURES' approved 5etools-mirror 2014-family source and complete profile verification before activation; do not invent 2024 mechanics, new abilities or hidden compensating changes.

## Regional story coverage and substantial rewards
Player direction **OOC-D278-ORATH-REGIONAL-DESIGN-001**, adopted 2026-11-21 (America/Los_Angeles): take inspiration from the requested Witcher 3 style of rich regional stories and independent quests. Apply across the campaign's regions, beginning with Orath; this is a design mandate, not a new itinerary or accepted quest.

- Give each substantial geographic region an overarching conflict with its own cast, discoverable causes, consequential approaches and a durable local ending. Larger countries need meaningful subregions (sides, interior, coast, frontier or watershed as geography warrants), not one capital plot standing for the entire country. Every established major city/capital also needs distinct coverage. Compact city-states use their supporting settlements/approaches and city; do not manufacture provinces, unknown cities or national borders to fill a grid.
- Connect regional arcs credibly to the main plots through actual people, history, institutions, resources, opportunities or consequences. A connection can supply refuge, influence, evidence authentication or a strategic alternative; it need not reveal the same villain everywhere. Preserve independent political disputes, monster ecology and local causes. Do not force ancestry ties, turn every quest-giver into bait, or merge already distinct conspiracies.
- Prepare independent substantial adventures from varied walks of life: workers, rural households, caregivers, artists, scholars, soldiers, merchants, religious communities, outsiders and elites. Vary hunting, defense, investigation, infiltration, exploration, recovery, rescue, pursuit, negotiation and strategic choices. Avoid reskinning the same trapped-workers, camp–depot–headquarters or paperwork sequence throughout the map.
- Significant adventures should have high personal/local/regional stakes and **substantial money or a worthwhile magical item** as a material reward, with credible ownership and clear terms. Ordinary kindness, relationships and quiet life may still stand alone. Do not routinely use gratitude as the only payoff for dangerous commissioned work. Agree on partial-success terms and honor them even when the wider arc is declined.
- Fix the payer's finite means and authority or the treasure's provenance before offering it. No raid on player estates, invented market stock, retroactive bounty, automatic heirloom ownership or player resource change from preparation. Verify an exact permitted item's source, mechanics, custody and transfer conditions before promising it. Cash budgets are design proposals until a particular offer is grounded; do not treat reward ranges as current economic facts or a level-up formula.
- Keep early introductions useful: a specific source, tangible problem, understandable stakes, practical first step, disclosed travel implications and reward. Essential answers have independent paths; the quest may change shape when its truth emerges. Avoid mandatory moral ambiguity: deliberate atrocities can be clearly evil, while innocent people and competing legitimate interests retain individuality.
- Regional coverage is a persistent design responsibility, not a daily creation quota or a requirement to play everything. Default to fully preparing the current/committed region and lighter alternatives, but honor explicit broader preparation. OOC-D278-ORATH-PREP-NOW-001 ('Why not prepare them now?') authorizes executable preparation across the existing Orath coverage now: do not leave distant causes, routes, rewards, profiles or resolutions blank solely because V has not chosen them. Preparation still grants no accepted quest, travel or reward. Completed victories and declined jobs stay completed/declined; new work requires a genuinely distinct cause.

The private index routes coverage and the next development cursor. [Orath regional owner](../../prep/dm-only/dm-orath-regional-arcs.md) holds the first continent pass and exact gaps; individual existing outlines remain authoritative for their established adventures. A continent framework is not a blanket G2/G3 pass.

## V's campaign spine: rediscovery, a lasting house and an interconnected epic
Player direction **OOC-EPIC-CAMPAIGN-SPINE-20261124-001**: V's main story is discovering his past and building a lasting house. It is intended as a long-running, far-ranging epic, with natural openings into other main and minor plots and scope capable of growing from rediscovering a laboratory to confronting a government, secret society or god. This is an affirmative campaign-design mandate, not a commitment by V to a particular war, throne or kill.

- **Keep both central pursuits active.** Reliable discoveries about V's life, origins, relationships, losses and inheritance should inform what he wants to build now. Building a house includes people, belonging, legitimacy, reputation, alliances, places, institutions, resources and continuity beyond personal adventuring. Neither pursuit is merely a side reward of the other; practical household development and personal relationships can be meaningful parts of the epic. A house's future form, succession and values remain V's choices and other people's voluntary commitments.
- **Weave plots through earned relationships and causes.** Major arcs, regional conflicts, companion interests and selected smaller adventures should regularly intersect these pursuits through evidence, shared stakes, informed people, access, obligations, assets or the consequences of earlier choices. Different origins can lead to meaningful intersection. P8's independence rule protects distinct causes; it does not require isolated episodes with no lasting connection. Important connections must become understandable and actionable once sufficient evidence is earned.
- **Build cumulative chapters.** A completed chapter should settle a real question or objective, create a durable gain/change, and leave a credible opening toward a larger question or opportunity where appropriate. Give concrete answers as investigation earns them; do not withhold V's entire past until a distant finale or manufacture ambiguity just to prolong play. Personal revelations, political standing, allies, recovered knowledge and a stable home can carry forward alongside substantial monetary/magical rewards.
- **Prepare genuine escalation in scale.** A local site can expose a bounded operation; evidence and allies can make its wider institution reachable; sustained accomplishments can support regional power struggles and eventual institutional, sovereign or divine-scale objectives. These are overlapping possible horizons, not a compulsory ladder, mandatory sequence or level-up spawn rule. Larger actors, their resources and relevant relationships must have credible prior existence and discoverable limits. Taking down an established government, dismantling a secret society or confronting a god must be capable of becoming a real playable undertaking and ending, rather than forever receding behind new superiors or protected villains.
- **Let methods scale with the ambition.** Later objectives may require expeditions, research, allies, access, logistics, rival factions, battlefield or covert action and specific magical vulnerabilities as well as personal power. Set concrete intermediate objectives and genuine alternatives; a government is not merely one larger stat block. A god's vulnerability must follow this world's fixed divinity and verified target-specific preparation, not an automatic god-killing device. Preserve party agency, fair threats and the possibility of choosing a different end.
- **Preserve closure within continuity.** Completing a laboratory rescue, local liberation or other chapter remains a real victory even if its wider institution survives. Follow-up opportunities grow from what actually remains and who learns what; they do not annul the accomplishment. V may stop, defer or change priorities. Long duration comes from consequential development, relationships, varied regions and substantial adventures, not filler, repeat rescues, endless withheld clues or compulsory sequels.
- **Plan broad horizons and prepare the next real decision.** The existing standing-story and long-arc owners hold the connective campaign plan; individual outlines own exact truth, delivery and execution. Keep distant chapters as meaningful goals/questions and source routes, with honest readiness; develop the selected arc fully before consequential approach. Current core threads must receive substantive opportunities and progress, not be perpetually displaced by unrelated small jobs. Quiet returns, cultural life and successful periods of building belong in the same epic.
- **Review the weave explicitly.** For a substantial chapter record its central question/objective, contribution to rediscovery and/or the lasting house (or a deliberate independent role), connections to relevant other threads, what becomes known/possible after success, independent routes onward, durable local endpoint and next compatible source. Do not make every local monster an ancestor's experiment or every useful contact a conspirator. Preserve established truth, accepted victories and the real scope of each actor's knowledge.

## Living companions and visible, consequential villains
Player direction **OOC-COMPANION-VILLAIN-PRESENCE-20261124-001** extends the epic spine: companions have needs, wants and self-directed stories; villains recur visibly, provoke earned emotional investment and usually culminate in interesting combat.

### Companions are protagonists in their own lives
- Give each active companion a present want, longer ambition, personal relationship/obligation, need or vulnerability, boundary and something they can initiate. Ground these in existing biography and accepted play. Let joy, curiosity, affection, pride, faith, recovery and ordinary personal pursuits matter alongside danger; not every need becomes a rescue quest.
- Companions initiate concrete proposals, requests, conversations, investigation and feasible personal activities without waiting for V to ask. They can lead their own quest, make judgments, disagree, negotiate shared priorities and pursue independent relationships. Portray exchanges between companions as well as with V; do not reduce them to supportive commentary, combat abilities or messengers for his objectives.
- Personal quests may fall within, intersect or run parallel to V's rediscovery/house story. Connect through established history, shared interests, relationships, evidence or consequences. Parallel stories deserve substantial development and closure even without a hidden link to V.
- Keep a compact owner record: want/need; actual knowledge; current voluntary commitment; independent next move and means; actionable request/first step; compatible scene trigger; response to help, postponement or refusal; possible payoff/change. The DM decides the companion's behavior in actual play; preparation does not record an action as already taken. No offscreen spending from V, compulsory itinerary, automatic romance, forced reconciliation or invented incoming letter.
- At natural shared scenes, make an eligible companion initiative visible. Waiting for the player to solicit every personal goal is a delivery defect. Allow ordinary activity and quiet relationship scenes without constantly interrupting them with emergencies. A companion may accept postponement, continue a feasible personal step or disagree for a grounded reason; refusal is not an automatic betrayal or loyalty test.

### Villains need presence and a reason to stop them
- Use a small recognizable cast of existing major antagonists and distinctive operational foes. Give each a concrete harmful objective, chosen conduct, recognizable voice/method, finite means, vulnerability and attainable defeat condition. Establish emotional stakes through people and places the party can understand, not merely a threatening title or secret dossier.
- Prepare meaningful repeated presence: an actual encounter/parley, witnessed orders or conduct, a credible victim/associate, an identifiable message, a public move, evidence of a specific act, or a supported reaction to the party. Each appearance/mention should add recognition, knowledge, changed stakes or counterplay; repeated anonymous rumors and empty taunts are not development. Use direct contact when plausible, rather than keeping every villain offscreen until death.
- Make the need to oppose them concrete through threatened interests, deliberate atrocities, coercion, destruction or ongoing lethal action. Moral complexity can concern their rationale, allies and aftermath while stopping their violence remains clearly justified. Do not dictate V's emotions, use repeated loved-one kidnappings as the default, or retcon unrelated losses to manufacture hatred.
- Recurrence is an intention for preparation, never plot armor. Establish presence, communications, defenses and possible escape methods before contact. If the party earns an early kill/capture, honor it and its consequences; no automatic resurrection, invulnerable monologue, forced surrender refusal or new superior invented to replace the defeated villain. Actual survivors remember only evidenced losses/news.
- Review the selected major arc's last actual recognizable villain contact/mention and next source-eligible presence beat. If the antagonist has remained abstract through a substantial chapter, repair delivery in upcoming play. No fixed encounter quota, omniscient cutaway or newly completed offscreen harm merely to increase screen time.

### Major villain climaxes: combat as the usual prepared payoff
- **Normally prepare a decisive, memorable combat culmination for major villains.** Give the party the opportunity to confront and defeat the responsible antagonist, with a concrete result when they win. This is the default experience target, not a compulsory fight or immunity to negotiation, infiltration, sabotage, assassination or another earned early victory.
- Build the battle around the villain's project and the environment: interrupting an established ritual, shutting down an experiment, protecting captives while attacking the operator, breaking a source of protection, seizing a moving objective, or preventing an escape while containing a hazard. Pick what belongs to this villain; do not give everyone the same ritual or mandatory three-phase battle.
- Transformations and changing phases must be source-supported creature abilities, existing approved effects or explicitly authorized homebrew with complete rules. Predeclare trigger, warning, action cost, finite forms/HP/resources, interaction with conditions/concentration, weaknesses and how the change can be prevented or exploited. Never refill HP or invent immunity/reinforcements because the party won too fast. Environmental phases can create drama without modifying the creature.
- A ritual/experiment packet needs the process and operator, visible progress cues, exact clock and what advances it, available actions/targets/DCs/AC/HP, concentration/line-of-effect rules where applicable, multiple interruption methods, finite opposition, rescue/retreat routes, partial outcomes and definite shutdown/defeat. An unexplained countdown or a required single skill check is insufficient.
- G3 covers the complete encounter including phases, hazards and objective actions using the approved 2014-family source procedure. Story approval alone is not mechanical readiness. Telegraph danger and preserve actual party resources, rest rules and finite enemy assets. Rewards and lasting consequences match the achieved objective, including clever prevention of the intended finale.

## Campaign tone: power, peril, culture and wonder
Player direction OOC-QUEST-STRUCTURE-TONE-20261124-001: the New Unnamed World should evoke **Game of Thrones, The Witcher 3 and Faerûn**, through high-level political theater, dangerous wilds and unexpectedly hostile creatures associated with the Convergence, rich cultural interaction and fantastically magical elements. These are tonal/design influences within this world's established history, institutions and2014/homebrew rules; no imported setting continuity or copied plots.

- **Consequential political theater.** Courts, councils, great houses, military commands, temples, cities and independent minor communities pursue concrete interests with finite influence and means. Stage important audiences, assemblies, public vows, negotiations, rival claims, alliances and betrayals with atmosphere and personal stakes. Succession, war, legitimacy, supply, faith and control of magical assets can matter on a regional or national scale. Powerful actors can be competent, principled, loyal, fearful or self-serving; betrayal and corruption are not inevitable. V can influence a bounded outcome without accepting office, pledging troops or settling an entire country's future. Advance politics through genuine stakes and decisions, not a chain of routine clerical permissions.
- **Dangerous, inhabited wilds.** Beyond maintained routes lie consequential terrain, predators, undead, hostile magical creatures and sites whose dangers are not neatly matched to party level. The Convergence joined ecosystems and ancient places from different worlds about1,200 years ago; unfamiliar combinations, territorial conflicts and persistent magical pockets can support surprising danger. Fix a particular creature's habitat, motive/behavior, source profile, limits and local cause before contact. Surprise need not mean an arbitrary new power or an enemy moved onto V's chosen road. Preserve real detection/ambush rules, available warning signs, avoidance/retreat, regional encounter tables, caps and quiet days. No new daily Convergence or ongoing portal-spawning mechanism is implied.
- **Rich cultural interaction.** Show languages, faith, living Convergence memory, food, craft, art, festivals, guest customs, class, kinship, law and rival historical accounts through people doing ordinary things. Cultural differences can create trust, friction, humor, obligations and unexpected cooperation, not only exposition or prejudice checks. Individuals differ within every culture; local customs and institutions materially affect access, testimony and choices. Adopted minor entities are real societies, not decorative labels or interchangeable quest boards.
- **Fantastical magic and wonder.** Give the world visible marvels as well as danger: extraordinary landscapes and creatures, enchanted places, ancient works, arcane traditions, sacred remains, magical crafts and institutions, and remarkable treasures where canon supports them. Present sensory wonder, mystery, beauty and practical social effects—not only magic as enemy attacks or rare shop listings. Develop still-open places and adventure situations to carry this texture. Preserve established finite mortal magical reserves/recharge, unequal specialist access, dead-god limits, rare dragons, no healing potions and actual item custody; atmosphere is not a free mechanical effect, unlimited spell service or guaranteed stock. Specify any consequential new effect under owning rules before use.
- **Variety and contrast across play.** The campaign should recurrently deliver substantial political drama, dangerous exploration/monster combat, lived culture and magical wonder. Do not require all four in every quest or every day. Warmth, friendship, leisure, ordinary competence, hard-won safety and durable victories make the dangerous and spectacular moments matter. A polity can connect to a main plot through resources, knowledge, refuge or consequences without sharing a universal conspiracy or ancestry link.

World foundations and the relevant political/atlas owners govern actual facts; this mandate guides new/OPEN DESIGN and live presentation. It does not retcon completed scenes, alter fixed opposition, introduce a current magical phenomenon, activate an encounter or advance independent actors. When an existing record cannot support an intended effect, identify the exact design gap and prepare it in its proper owner.

## Journey design: the route is playable space
Apply when a destination implies meaningful travel, particularly a journey lasting weeks. Prepare the route alongside the destination; do not leave it as elapsed days plus random fights. Use the existing adventure owner or one linked corridor owner, not another public quest tracker.

- **Ground the corridor:** established endpoints, intermediate anchors, direction, jurisdictions, terrain, transport suitability, approximate duration and uncertainty. Distinguish known ordinary roads from genuinely unknown access. A missing inn name or measured mileage does not make an established trade road unusable. Explain major optional detour/time tradeoffs before commitment; do not promise exact costs or dates without support.
- **Give travel dramatic opportunities:** consider a substantial optional local adventure, an existing plot contact where causally eligible, exploration and ordinary culture/relationships. For a weeks-long committed route prepare one strong candidate and a light alternative where useful; this is preparation depth, not a required encounter count. Tie causes to local livelihoods, ecology, politics and existing roads. Some opportunities may be independent of the destination or ancestry.
- **Make the opening actionable:** an informed traveler, visible circumstance, reachable local contact or established notice can explain what is happening, why help matters, the practical first step and likely diversion. Locate the source and account for report age. Do not require V to ask OOC for a road quest or search every settlement. Offer at the first compatible actual approach/contact, without stacking crises or interrupting another substantial scene.
- **Make it an adventure:** objective, people with differing interests, finite opposition/hazards, meaningful approaches, discoverable truths, refusal/retreat and a complete local payoff. Consider combat, social, puzzle and exploration without forcing all four. No trivial permit chain, obligatory escort contract, road closure manufactured to force acceptance, or hidden prerequisite for the destination.
- **Plan the return conditionally:** record observable branches after help, compromise, refusal or no contact; changed relationships/access and grounded aftermath may make revisiting worthwhile. Specify who knows what and how information travels. A cleared site stays cleared unless an independently caused change occurs. Return can be peaceful and shorter at the table; no mandatory second crisis.
- **Keep travel scale honest:** preserve homebrew rests, supply/animal needs, actual party and the cost of optional days away. Quiet days and ordinary warmth matter. For Nightglass, the outbound 3–4-week estimate and similar return can mean 6–8 weeks of baseline travel before visits, chosen diversions or recovery, not a fixed script.
- **Separate random travel and authored situations:** RULES TRAVEL owns occurrence dice, regional tables, caps and quiet-day protections. Authored opportunities require independent causes, location, information and eligibility; they are never relabeled random filler to evade those protections. Do not arbitrarily raise risk because travel needs excitement.
- **Freeze only at the right point:** distant candidates remain explicitly unplayed preparation; before their first clue/decision, fix location, incident timing, involved actors, finite threat and meaningful response window. Once established, preserve them even if V changes route or arrives later. No moving the same adventure to whichever road V chooses, reverse-engineering danger from a roll, or resetting a rescue clock on return.

Compact route packet: corridor/known anchors; duration and alternatives; source and next delivery opportunity; substantial candidate/closure; local culture and quiet space; actual rest/supply implications; outward/return conditional changes; independent plot ties or explicit separation; scoped G1/G2/G3 and next preparation trigger. Review departure, meaningful route changes, relevant new information and return planning. Real-world recurrence alone changes no conditions.

## Three readiness gates
Use **PASS / CONDITIONAL / HOLD / NOT REVIEWED / N/A** separately for each gate, with an owner-section reference, substantive evidence, concrete missing item and next work trigger. PASS certifies the named scope only; a model's self-review is not playtesting. N/A needs a reason. No numeric “quality score” substitutes for evidence.

| Gate | Apply when | Evidence needed to pass |
|---|---|---|
| G1 Narrative integrity | Before substantial design | Premise/promise; conflict and actors; grounded geography/history/culture; fixed relevant causes; finite local payoff; consistent larger connections or explicit independence; for a substantial quest, several connected situations with meaningful development rather than a single encounter presented as a full adventure |
| G2 Playability and agency | Before offering the relevant playable situation | Credible source and delivery opportunity; understandable objective/stakes; feasible first step; known travel/access implications before commitment; meaningful alternatives; essential revelation paths and viable routes through important progression points; concrete outcomes/retreat; public/secret knowledge separated; substantial-quest scope distinguished from entry/encounter scope |
| G3 Encounter and resolution readiness | Before the particular consequential encounter or conclusion | Complete applicable statistics/rules, layout/positions/senses, finite opposition, predeclared checks/hazards, alarm/reaction logic, danger/resource/rest assessment, alternatives and consequence/reward/custody handling |

Scope gates to the decision being made: a credible social invitation can pass G2 before a remote dungeon passes G3. Incomplete route facts block a proposed departure itinerary, not a conversation about interest. Incomplete tactical details block that confrontation, not the entire campaign. Never hide a known shortcoming with an unqualified READY label. Once a gate passes, reopen only for changed evidence, new relevant choice or a material defect; no repeated full audit of unchanged prep.

## Compact owning adventure record
Use this structure within the existing owning outline; retain links to detailed mechanics instead of copying whole trackers. Small scenes can use a paragraph. Substantial quests normally need a short front summary plus only relevant detailed nodes.

Premise/promise; context; stakes; actors (objective/knowledge/means/constraints); underlying truth and uncertainty; player opportunities/delivery; situation/progression network (what each node changes, routes onward, fallbacks and possible early closure); discovery network; locations; challenges/alternatives; escalation and act/wait/stop triggers; resolutions/rewards; connections and independent causes; contribution to V's rediscovery/lasting house or deliberate independent role; chapter payoff and actionable onward openings; readiness G1/G2/G3.

For each essential revelation record: answer, independent clues/methods and locations, what each proves/does not prove, ordinary discovery versus uncertainty checks, and fallback after missed/destroyed evidence. For each connection record: causal reason, source evidence, who can know, actual reveal point, actionable continuation, effect of disclosure and local closure if declined. Once sufficient evidence is earned, explain the connection plainly in play; do not leave it as recurring “familiar symbols.”

For each imminent encounter record: objective/uncertainty, source-backed opposition/behavior, terrain/geometry, entry and detection, checks/hazards fixed before use, alternatives, failure/retreat and aftermath. A puzzle design is not a substitute for its hazardous execution mechanics.

## Revision boundaries
1. **Accepted play / player-known facts / revealed clues / fixed opponent or spatial facts relied on by choices:** preserve. Correct evidenced factual drift; do not silently redesign or reroll. If incompatible with a proposed improvement, change the proposal.
2. **Already committed hidden truth/roster:** preserve unless a specific player ruling authorizes revision or a real in-world cause changes it. “Unplayed” alone is not permission to rewrite fixed answers after player investigation.
3. **OPEN DESIGN and future contingent scenes:** develop/restructure freely within established canon and this mandate. Merge concepts only if causes, player commitments and independent closures survive.
4. **Authoritative canon or specialist data conflicts:** hold the narrow dependent design and route a precise issue to its owner. Do not alter estate money, personal resources, market stock, public atlas or established politics to make a quest work.
5. **No adversarial retuning:** never strengthen opposition, erase a bypass, add a sudden deadline or move a site in response to V succeeding.
6. **Do not backfill scenes:** planned invitations, world reactions and prep revisions are not deliveries, discoveries or accepted quests. Distinguish newly authored private fictional background from recovered historical evidence.

## Recurring Quest Development workflow
Apply on the existing task schedule; real-world recurrence never advances campaign time.

1. **Recover and reconcile:** read current owning checkpoint, relevant accepted changes/receipts, current delivery queue and scoped outlines. Correct supported public/private status drift first. Inspect semantic changes since prior review; keep checkpoint ownership singular. No broad estate/market/archive rereads without relevance.
2. **Delivery before invention:** review the 2–4 candidate shortlist, informed contacts, next opportunities, unresolved promises and skipped eligible openings. Complete missing DM introduction work. A player question about direction is a trigger even in an unchanged room. One skipped eligible opening gets priority at the next compatible one or a concrete reason/next trigger. A scheduled run prepares this handoff; only live play delivers it.
3. **Choose work by need:** first material continuity/agency defects and imminent G2/G3 blockers; then weak active/offered quests, overlooked substantial arcs, selected travel corridors (outward and return) and accepted player interests; then a bounded rotation of older open/unintroduced quest records. Normally review 1–3 substantial owners per run, more only for causal dependencies or a major change. This is an efficiency default, not a cap that leaves necessary work half done.
4. **Apply the framework and revise:** Read adjacent played scenes for what V actually spent table time doing. If several center on briefings, permissions, handoffs or reports, look at what the existing situation lets V experience firsthand. A disputed crossing might matter through a family trying to use it. Leave room for curiosity, ordinary company and passing by. Keep earned obligations and deadlines real, and compress routine follow-through within V’s authorization. Identify specific defects by U/P/Q/S/E ID; repair eligible prep directly, improve discovery/choice/payoff, remove redundancy, clarify intentional links and preserve independent threads. Check declared scale: a substantial quest needs development and alternative progression, not just encounter approaches. Assess whether the eligible slate advances V's rediscovery/lasting-house spine through earned connections and supplies political theater, dangerous wilds/clear lethal opposition, cultural interaction and magical wonder across play; record specific gaps without imposing a four-part quota on each quest. Complete nearby mechanical prep when needed, not endless narrative polishing. If a blocked fact cannot be resolved, retain the exact dependent claim and next action; continue independent work.
5. **New quest decision:** create only for a concrete gap—region or player interest unsupported, insufficient credible deliverable substantial choices, missing distinct experience, or an established consequence requiring a new situation. Compare existing candidates first. Rework/expand/merge when that solves the gap. No daily content quota; zero new quests is valid. A genuinely useful substantial outline may be created as a new private Markdown owner and registered once in the existing index; no parallel public trackers.
6. **Gate and regression review:** verify relevant G1/G2/G3 evidence, not blanket completion. Test a direct question, an alternate approach, refusal/withdrawal, a failed important check, loss of a clue/NPC, early success and local closure without sequel. For a substantial quest, also trace at least two viable ways through important progression points, one failed/missing route, and an early-success path; state what materially changes between situations. Identify any unjustified moral penalty for defeating clearly established hostile opposition. Check actual geography, causal/knowledge timing, rules, finite assets and future-plot compatibility. Do not fabricate dice for testing a design.
7. **Save and hand off:** replace/refine the relevant owning sections rather than append another full audit essay. Keep only short current gate/defect/next-work entries in the private index and real changes in owning private history where needed. Preserve accepted history and source provenance. Use repo expected-SHA/guarded writes, verify files and commits, then recheck checkpoint. Update public QUESTS/NPCS only from actual accepted evidence; do not touch CAMPAIGN_STATE for design-only work.
8. **Report narrowly:** brief spoiler-safe changes, ready-to-deliver scope, unresolved material blockers, and whether anything new was created. Remain quiet on no material change. Do not disclose hidden titles, villains, answers, clue locations or triggers. A scheduled receipt cannot mean the player received a quest.

## Review coverage and stopping rules
The private index holds one compact current review ledger, not a daily report dump:
`owner | last reviewed checkpoint + relevant content/commit reference | scope | G1/G2/G3 | material defect/action | next review trigger`.
Retain a rotation cursor and unrevised backlog only where needed. Prioritize due/imminent work; otherwise choose the next open owner not yet reviewed under QDF-1. Closed quests are excluded except evidence-backed aftermath or a distinct new event. New generation must not starve old review.

Do not rewrite every outline in one run, append the same verdict on an unchanged day, manufacture urgency to justify activity, or treat unchanged campaign time as proof no creative improvement is needed. At the same checkpoint, finish genuinely unresolved prep or the next bounded initial-framework review; stop when that scope is sufficient. Record honest partial coverage; do not claim all campaign quests passed from reviewing one.

## Failure patterns to reject
Railroading; illusory choices; endlessly withheld answers; escalation by uncaused threats; contrived universal conspiracies; reactive cheating; redundant scenes; empty endings; unrequested expansion that crowds out imminent work; and preparation that never reaches play. Review is useful only when it improves a playable opportunity or resolves a material defect.
