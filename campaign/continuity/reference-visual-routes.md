## Operational visual preflight — approved 17 Nov 2026
This section implements, not supersedes, the established visual-presentation and individual gallery rules below. If so, inspect the current corresponding live Google Drive folder and verify the selected file still exists; prefer the mood-appropriate extant portrait, otherwise the definitive face/base reference. (2) Is a new important settlement, chamber, terrain feature, monster, significant object or discovery being introduced? If so, retrieve relevant existing external/reference art or the official map where appropriate. (3) Present one or a small appropriate group of images near the scene's establishing description when supported; if private Drive images cannot be embedded, provide a link to the specific verified file rather than replacing the character with generic web art. Never describe a fetched atmospheric image as a canon-accurate portrait or proof of an unplayed outfit, action or event. (4) Do not refetch within an unchanged dialogue/combat beat; reassess when the visual subject or location meaningfully changes. No arbitrary quota or image use that interferes with gameplay.
## Scene-matching taxonomy — reusable rather than literal (17 Nov 2026)
Tag approved portraits by SCENE FUNCTION first, not a unique literal room, event, outfit or pose. An image may serve many situations. Use one dominant category plus relevant secondary categories and a mood. Reusable categories: (A) Study/Discovery — reading, evidence, questioning, explaining; (B) Travel/Exploration — roads, outdoors, ruins, curiosity; (C) Watchfulness/Action — scouting, stealth, pursuit, dangerous readiness; (D) Public/Social — town, market, tavern, introductions, gathering; (E) Quiet/Reflective — concern, grief, private thought, difficult decisions; (F) Warm/Playful — humor, camaraderie, relaxed teasing, celebration; (G) Formal/Ceremonial — court, noble duties, reception, remembrance as appropriate; (H) Private/Affectionate — intimate talks, closeness or romance ONLY when established by the actual scene. These are retrieval tags, not assertions that a pictured event happened or that each character has every category.
Match in this order: (1) correct approved character and surviving LIVE file [mandatory]; (2) dominant scene function and compatible emotion/expression [highest]; (3) pose or general activity; (4) broad backdrop (indoor/outdoor; town/wilderness; daylight/night); (5) clothing, props and exact architecture [low]. Time, pose and setting should be close enough to feel plausible, but need not literally equal the current location. Do not use a strikingly contradictory expression or context (e.g., laughing while grieving, romantic boudoir for court testimony, active combat pose for peaceful reading). If a scene has mixed moods, match the emotion of the pictured character, not automatically V's mood. Prioritize a strong overall fit over matching the filename; break plausible ties by avoiding repetition. A near match remains an illustrative character reference, not a depiction of a canon exact moment.
Use broad retrieval families within the EXISTING live indexes (examples, not exclusive pairings): Mara—study 01/03/07, travel/support 04/08, public/warm 02/05, reflection 06/10, R-series only for actually fitting private/formal/romantic scenes; Fenar—watchful 09/17/19/28, travel 12/22/26, public/playful 08/20/25/30, reflective 15/21/23, 01–06 only for fitting romantic/private scenes; Elara—public/travel 13/15, thoughtful/private 01/07, formal 03/10, mourning 12/14, recreational fencing 11, evening/warm 04/05/16, private 02/06/09 only when warranted. Consult current gallery descriptions and live roster before committing a file: filenames or this mnemonic are not evidence that it still exists. Prefer the current selected face reference (Fenar 13; Mara/Elara 00) plus separate environmental art if all scene variants visibly clash.
Show the most suitable portrait near the scene description when the display capability supports it; a verified direct Drive link is the honest fallback when private Drive files cannot display inline. Do not claim that a link embedded an image. At a meaningful emotional pivot or scene-function change, select a different appropriate variant; otherwise keep the established image for that scene. No quota and no images on every combat turn/dialogue beat. Do not generate new campaign images without a new explicit ruling.
## Portrait tone tiers — standard / romantic / sensual (17 Nov 2026)
Each approved image has TWO independent selection dimensions: the reusable SCENE FUNCTION above (study, travel, watchfulness, public, quiet, warm, formal, private) and a TONE TIER. Tiers govern appropriateness, not relationship status or mechanical intimacy rules.
S0 STANDARD (default): everyday, travel, research, public, court, friendly/playful, ordinary domestic/private and serious/emotional scenes without romantic or sexual emphasis. Select S0 even when V is talking privately to a lover about duties, danger, grief or ordinary life. Glamour/formal dress alone is not automatically sexy.
R1 ROMANTIC / AFFECTIONATE: a played scene has a clear flirtatious, courtship, tender or affectionate tone; choose a fitting smile, gaze, pose and atmosphere. This includes nonsexual closeness and is not permission to select overt boudoir/bathing/sexualized imagery.
S2 SENSUAL / SEXY: deliberately sensual or sexy scene with an established adult, consensual context and a participating character, where a noticeably provocative pose, intimate clothing, bath, bedchamber, reclining or boudoir image actually fits what is happening. Do not switch to S2 merely from the identity of the characters, previous relationships, being alone, casual teasing or the presence of a bedroom. Images do not initiate/authorize V's acts, infer intimacy, force NPC reciprocation or add unplayed undressing, contact or sexual activity. DM retains NPC autonomy and V's choices belong solely to the player.
SELECTION GATE: First classify the current played scene's tone S0/R1/S2; then retrieve only images at or below that scene's suitable visual intensity. Allow S0 as an identity fallback in R1/S2; R1 may accompany S2 where tone fits. Never use R1/S2 for S0 just because that image matches an unrelated setting or costume. Within the allowed tier use the established likeness > emotional expression and scene function > pose > approximate setting > exact clothing hierarchy. An ordinary/private/quiet scene is NOT S2 by default. When uncertain, choose the lower tier; do not ask for tone confirmation just to display an image. At scene transitions reevaluate and de-escalate visual tier when the scene changes.

## Internet-sourced visual references — same frequency and appropriateness standards (17 Nov 2026)
Web-fetched existing art is subject to the SAME proactive scene-start/scene-change visual preflight, scene-function taxonomy, expression/mood/pose compatibility, and no-repetition pacing as saved NPC art. Trigger an image search for a newly encountered important locale (city district, castle, ruin, chamber, wilderness terrain), a significant creature/monster or encounter, or a visually meaningful object/discovery, whenever an illustration would help the player picture the scene. Do not wait for an explicit request or silently omit searches just because the source is the internet. For a sustained unchanged scene, retain existing art; refresh when the focus, threat, environment or emotional tone materially changes, not every dialogue/combat beat. No fixed image quota.
Before searching, identify the depiction target and 2–4 grounded visual characteristics from known/revealed fiction: subject/creature identity, terrain or architecture, time/light, scale, activity, atmosphere. Use those to make a focused image-search query; inspect likely candidates for meaningful compatibility. Choose broad reusable matches, not images that must literally reenact the scene. Prioritize (1) correct monster/subject category and era/setting, (2) scene function and action/threat, (3) emotional atmosphere/light, (4) overall terrain/architectural resemblance, (5) incidental details. Reject visually misleading subjects (wrong monster, wrong biome, advanced technology, cheerful tavern in a funeral scene, artwork that reveals something not actually known). A plausible neutral landscape is better than a dramatic wrong one.
Tone matters equally for web art: STANDARD imagery by default, romantic ambiance only for established romance, and sensual references only for an actually sensual adult scene; don't escalate visual tone based on location or character relationship alone. Use existing approved LIVE Drive portraits for canonical Mara/Fenar/Elara likeness; a generic web person must NEVER stand in as their actual face. Internet images may depict environment, monsters, gear, or anonymous background figures but remain illustrative, not proof of exact canon dimensions, layout, individuals, revealed secrets or events. Prefer one strong establishing image or a small nonredundant group, display inline where supported; otherwise link the specific retrieved source rather than claim it appeared. If no suitable image is found, continue without forcing a mismatched visual. This rule requires only retrieval of existing art, not image generation.
