# Historical timeline
Year numbers are a planning convention measured from the Convergence, not a universal calendar. Date ranges can overlap and indicate uneven regional processes rather than synchronized eras. Names such as the Broken Roads and Boundary Wars are later historians' shorthand. No single authority coordinated recovery. Maglubiyet's death and Ilyra Vask's accession remain undated.
| Approximate period | Event or development | Established consequences and limits |
| - | - | - |
| Before Year 0 | Tiamat's fall | Five Great Wyrms slay Tiamat; their wounds enable separate dragonborn captures. Vathrune becomes a pilgrimage center at her burial site. Exact dates and motives remain open. |
| Year 0 | The Convergence | Territories, peoples, gods, institutions, and ecosystems from different worlds are joined into a previously human world. |
| Years 0–10 | The Broken Roads | Communities reopen severed routes, negotiate with unfamiliar neighbors, and abandon some isolated settlements. Replacement routes elevate previously minor towns. |
| Years 0–30 | The Boundary Wars | Numerous local conflicts over overlapping fields, water, settlements, and inherited claims leave displacement and unresolved borders; not one worldwide war. |
| Years 0–50 | The Harbor Compacts | Coastal authorities negotiate pilotage, shipwreck assistance, market access, and recognition of merchants. Selected ports become intermediaries between previously unrelated societies. |
| Later first century | The First Pass Agreements | Aundar and Kheldorn sponsor refuges, repairs, and protected markets along their new corridor, preparing the ground for independent upland settlements. |
| Years 100–180 | The Corridor Settlements | Refuges grow into holds, farms, and workshops. Settlements cooperate over defense and tolls while resisting either ancestral crown's control. |
| Years 120–220 | The Reopened Sea Routes | Dependable seasonal voyages connect selected ports on all three continents. They help sustain Talassar's cross-sea holdings without fixing their acquisition dates. |
| Years 150–250 | The Schools of Exchange | Native human patrons support instruction by transplanted practitioners through apprenticeships, courts, temples, and negotiated residence. Access is uneven, with both elite and less exclusive traditions. |
| Around Year 250 | Bane's overthrow | Another god catastrophically wounds Bane. Civilians, soldiers, and defectors kill him when he retreats expecting aid: the First Mortal Godslaying. His divine opponent remains unidentified. |
| Years 250–350 | Veyr's Founding Settlement | Military leaders preserve Bane's administrative machinery and formalize Commonwealth government. Civilian claims to the rebellion's promises persist beneath entrenched military guardianship. |
| Years 300–400 | The Union of Seven Holds | Corridor settlements unite as independent Talveren rather than accept either crown's sovereignty. Aundar and Kheldorn eventually recognize it through negotiated trade and defense arrangements. |
| Years 300–450 | The New Accord Members | The Unbarred March, Bellwether Coast, and Wandering Wood establish governments and join the older alliance, producing six members. Separate founding dates and forest gnome divergence remain open. |
| Years 400–500 | The First Tomb Guarantees | Dragonborn states renew older protection of Vathrune's independence and pilgrimage access after disputes over custody and precedence. The precipitating dispute and whether fighting occurred remain open. |
| Years 500–600 | The Serevan Water Disputes | Zafiran estates, upland communities, and royal officials contest diversions and maintenance. Particular settlements leave broader authority unresolved, creating grievances later inherited by Oathmark. |
| Years 550–650 | The War of the Three Landings | With essential local allies, Talassar defeats coastal rivals seeking to expel it from several Namarra harbors. Supporting port families gain privileges the crown later struggles to restrain. |
| Years 600–700 | The Failed Western Campaign | Veyr withdraws from occupied western settlements after resistance, supply failures, and temporary neighboring cooperation. The alliance later fractures over costs and recovered territory. Participants and battlefield geography remain open. |
| Around Year 700 | Allhaven's Charter | Trading settlements and resident communities unite under a negotiated charter. Protected founding representation later becomes both safeguard and contested privilege. |
| Years 750–825 | The Disputed Sanctuary | A Veyran expedition identifies divine power in a neighboring sanctuary. Custodians refuse removal; a campaign captures the site but damages essential works. Later expeditions prioritize expertise and intact infrastructure. No additional Godslaying is implied; owner, source, and location remain open. |
| Years 800–900 | The Broken Granaries | Poor harvests in parts of Arden expose unequal access to stores. Relief, trade, migration, requisition, and debt produce differing outcomes, including shared reserves and land loss; not a continent-wide famine. |
| Around Year 900 | Lathander's final battle | Lathander dies. Survivors gather at his final sanctuary and establish Lastlight without ordinary clerical spellcasting. Enemy and precise circumstances remain open. |
| Years 900–950 | The Dawnrest Settlement | Founding communities, defenders, and sanctuary custodians negotiate Lastlight's shared government, turning emergency refuge into a durable institution. |
| Years 900–1150 | Accumulating mortal capability | Magical knowledge, artifacts, fragment research, and coordinated institutions develop unevenly; this is a long process rather than a single event. |
| Around Year 980 | Oathmark's separation | About 220 years before the present, frontier communities reject inadequate protection and disputed demands. Recognition leaves borders, water rights, and commercial access contested. |
| Around Year 1040 | Charter Coast independence | About 160 years before the present, resistance to naval taxes and trade restrictions produces a cross-sea republic. Talassar retains negotiated compensation and naval concessions. |
| Years 1050–1150 | The Contested Charters | Separate local struggles win written rights in exchange for taxes, loans, supplies, or service. Protections may exclude tenants, newcomers, and other land users. |
| Around Year 1120 | Crownsunder's separation | About 80 years before the present, a succession struggle divides Ossent's ancestral region, leaving rival claims and confiscated holdings. |
| Years 1150–1170 | The Frontier Settlements | The Dominion intensifies military settlement in older conquests using empowered cultivation and population growth. These provinces supply recruits and provisions while displaced communities persist. |
| Around Year 1170 | The emperor's accession | Gruumsh appoints the successful conqueror and organizer now reigning for roughly thirty years. Veteran networks and appointments reshape founding-clan relationships. |
| Years 1170–1190 | The Veyran Acquisition Drives | Successive commands intensify searches prompted by Bane's hand and acquisition of divine assets, linking expeditions, campaigns, and incorporation. |
| Years 1180–1195 | The Unfulfilled Defense Agreements | Threatened states exchange warnings, promises, and limited aid without dependable common commitments. Contacts and precedents survive without effective alliances. |
| Recent years | Godslayings and withdrawals | Different actors undertake independent Godslayings; divine withdrawals unsettle protection and diplomacy. Victims, perpetrators, sequence, and PC connections remain open. |
| Recent months | Frontier pressure | Localized defensive fighting and vicious incursions around Kheldorn; no established full invasion or siege of the great holds. |
| Weeks ago | Dunwall's occupation | Veyr suspends the city's council under emergency military administration after promising charter protection. Resistance is only beginning. |
| Around Year 1200 | Campaign present | Campaign opened with V at Eastwatch in Orath; the Continuity Pack owns the current position and recent play; Campaign Archive owns archived session blocks. |
Nobanion is confirmed among recent divine deaths (~six months before Day171), with no publicly verified killer, method or death site. V's and Cirren's divine parents remain unidentified to the protagonists; their killers and any common cause are not publicly established. Privately adjudicated facts belong only in the campaign's DM-only continuity, not this public world history. Other recent Godslayings remain open; Lathander's death ~300 years earlier is not a recent example.
