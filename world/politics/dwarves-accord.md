# The dwarven states
Two independent kingdoms from the same world arrived with longstanding trade, clan ties, intermarriage, shared religious traditions, and defense agreements. Hill dwarves follow Faerûn's gold dwarf foundation; mountain dwarves follow its shield dwarf foundation.
| State | Origin and government | Capital |
| - | - | - |
| Aundar | Ancestral gold dwarven kingdom within the Ambercrags; hereditary crown and prestigious clans | Embercrown, whose royal halls surround a vast forge |
| Kheldorn | Ancestral shield dwarven kingdom within the Ironspine; crown governing powerful fortified clan holds | Harrowdelve, a chasm city crossed by immense stone bridges |
| Talveren, formally the Seven Holds of Talveren | Founded after the Convergence by gold and shield dwarven settlers; founding clan council and elected high ruler | Kithring, seven founding holds joined around a meeting cavern |
The ancestral kingdoms lie within regular trading reach but require a substantial journey between them. Settlers established caravan refuges, mines, workshops, and permanent holds in intervening territory. Their holds united as independent Talveren. It controls important stretches of the connecting corridor, not necessarily every route or all intervening land.
Moradin is alive and actively fights to defend Kheldorn against the Iron Dominion. Current conflict consists of localized defensive skirmishes and vicious incursions around passes, disputed ground, surface farms, mining camps, and outlying holdings. There is no established full invasion or siege of the great holds.
Moradin's participation does not make him sovereign over all three states. Dwarven support is limited by distance, sovereignty, resources, and disagreements over obligations.
## Shared dwarven inheritance
Responsibility for things that outlast an individual connects Aundar, Kheldorn and Talveren. Makers and maintainers receive recognition; clans support injured members, dependents, apprentices and displaced kin while demanding contributions. Adoption and sworn incorporation are possible. Restitution can settle wrongs, though feuds persist. Food producers sustain the holds and deserve recognition not always granted.
| State | Adopted customs and tension |
| - | - |
| Aundar | Household tables display inherited work; apprenticeship presentations; stewardship accounts; negotiated marriage contributions. Prestige and ancestry compete with competence and newcomer ambition |
| Kheldorn | Neighborhood inspections, trained rescue obligations, return meals and places for displaced households. Protecting ancestral holdings can conflict with protecting their inhabitants |
| Talveren | Public infrastructure service contributes to standing; qualifications recognized across holds; founding seats and public compact readings. Settler independence can entrench privilege against later arrivals |
Long-lived inheritance does not make every dwarf a smith or a grudge-holder. Houses and households contain ordinary occupations and varied beliefs.
## Raukhar's Rest — Nobanion's feline Shifter descendants
Raukhar's Rest is a secluded mountain stronghold/community of about 80–120 people in Ilvaran's Silareth Ridges, predominantly dwarf-descended feline Shifters with a few other residents. The named Raukhar direct bloodline and extended kin inherit permanent feline ears/tails and Shifter traits from a distant child of Nobanion, intertwined with ancestors affected by lycanthropy; they age on a normal dwarven lifespan (~350 years), with family adulthood customs of their own. Their wider dwarven clan is distinct, unnamed and NOT sworn to Vaishnavi. House Vaishnavi awarded Raukhar's Rest to the family centuries ago as hereditary family property; it remains Raukhar-owned under local arrangements, not a sovereign state or V's automatic estate. Only the direct Raukhar service line historically supplied House Vaishnavi (across its generations and branches, not only V) with champions, personal guardians, hunters and trackers. Hedra Raukhar is matriarch; named family and private personalities are in Fenar's Roleplay tracker. Raukhar tradition honors deceased Nobanion, preserving an oath combining deep loyalty, competent judgment and individual choice. Nobanion's Godslaying ~six months before Day171 caused grief and uncertainty but not automatic divine power loss for everyone. Exceptionally potent hereditary demigod Fenar alone experienced Severing among named sisters; she is pursuing V but remains unintroduced. Additional clan politics, defenses and members remain open for actual play.
# The Open Table Accord
Halflings and gnomes came from the same world, arriving as a connected region. Two original halfling states and one rock gnomish state preserved a preexisting alliance. Two halfling states and one forest gnomish state formed afterward and joined it.
| State | Origin | Character and naming history |
| - | - | - |
| The Honeymeads | Original halfling | Rural commonwealth of villages, pasturelands, orchards, and communal beekeeping rights |
| The Crooked Bridges | Original halfling | League of river market towns founded around bridges on a winding river |
| The Unbarred March | New halfling | Frontier republic; its founding charter protects open roads and common land against hereditary enclosure |
| The Bellwether Coast | New halfling | Harbor state named for a headland bell guiding ships through fog |
| The Glass Warrens | Original rock gnomish | Federation of hillside towns, tunnels, glass-roofed courts, workshops, and observatories |
| The Wandering Wood | New forest gnomish | Autonomous woodland communities concealed by illusions that confuse paths and settlement locations |
Forest gnome traits developed rapidly in a branch of rock gnomes after the Convergence. Those communities later formed their state. The cause of their divergence remains open; magical environmental transformation is only a possible explanation.
The Open Table Accord preserves the sovereignty of its six members. Hosting rotates; no state has hereditary precedence. Its symbol is six different chairs around one table. It coordinates defense, trade, and disputes. Water use, tolls, land rights, and unequal defense burdens create disagreements.
Yondalla and Garl Glittergold are missing. Their clerics still receive spells, indicating they remain alive. Their locations, reasons for disappearing, and whether their disappearances are connected remain unknown.
## Shared Accord culture
Hospitality, practical contribution, reputation and keeping a place for others connect the six states. Humor challenges pretension but can exclude; competence earns influence while essential labor can be overlooked. Yondalla and Garl's absence increases dependence on communal habits without making every custom religious.
| State | Customs and cultural tension |
| - | - |
| Honeymeads | Managed hedges, orchards, commons and kitchen gardens. Walking bounds, spare plates, telling household news to bees and communal winter pantries. Care for members can exclude newcomers |
| Crooked Bridges | River markets, inns, kitchens and boatyards. Bridge sponsorship, trusted introductions, market performances and flood refuge. Reliable networks can restrict entry to trades |
| Unbarred March | Open-path inspections, communal raising days, watch suppers and grievance hearings. Settlers' charter freedoms may conflict with older land users |
| Bellwether Coast | Return meals, demanding pilot apprenticeships, shared boat ownership and fog watches. Shipowners need workers and pilots whose bargaining power they resist |
| Glass Warrens | Precision glasswork, instruments, balances and fittings. Demonstrations, annotated inherited tools and manuscripts, repair exchanges and witty disputations. Expertise can guard privilege and undervalue supporting labor |
| Wandering Wood | Managed clearings, gardens, coppices and hidden routes. Guided entry, guest paths, seasonal closures, indirect warnings and adopted neighbors. Protective concealment can control information and movement |
The forest gnomes' recent divergence complicates kinship with rock gnomes; shared ancestry does not settle whether they form a new identity. No state has one universal personality.
