# The elven states
Three of many kingdoms from a shared elven world arrived: high elven Ilvaran, wood elven Elderweald, and dark elven Vhalzareth. They share ancestral civilization and Faerûn-inspired history, including the Crown Wars, rather than forming three recently divided branches.
The Convergence scattered the states, severing many older diplomatic, trading, and hostile relationships. Distant connections survive, but local neighbors shape daily politics.
| State | Government and identity | Location |
| - | - | - |
| Ilvaran | High elven hereditary crown, ancient noble houses, magical institutions, and temples | Northwestern Orath, inland beyond Ossent |
| The Elderweald | Wood elven royal authority balanced by communities, clans, religious leaders, and local defenders | Namarra, inland and west of the Open Table Accord |
| Vhalzareth | Traditional Lolthite drow state, governed by competing matriarchal houses and priestesses | Beneath southern or southeastern Namarra, distant from both surface elven states |
Ilvaran and the Elderweald have a basis for genuine cooperation. Their relationships with Vhalzareth are hostile accommodations. A limited agreement recognizes envoys, practical trade, and protections for signatories' citizens; it is not an elven alliance.
Vhalzareth's slavery and religious oppression persist under the limited treaty; individual drow, escapees, and dissenters can reject the regime.
Corellon is alive and hiding. Both surface courts lack his location; his clerics still receive spells. His reasons and covert activities remain unresolved.
Lolth is alive, physically concealed in a fortified sanctuary, and actively governs through her priesthood and houses. She investigates Godslayings, seeks fragments and dangerous research, divides potential opponents, and limits infighting that threatens her defenses. She bargains selectively and prepares against imperial threats. She needs coordinated defenders while fearing their capacity to overthrow her. Cooperation with Gruumsh on any specific operation remains unestablished.
## Longevity and demographic vulnerability
Elven longevity supports accumulated expertise but not limitless capacity or automatic class levels. Mastery needs practice; long-held property and offices constrain younger adults.
Infrequent births and widely spaced generations make historical losses slow to replace across all elves. Communities may decline, stabilize, or recover; inevitable biological fading is not established.
Ilvaran's dominant response favors withdrawal and consolidation; the Elderweald favors concealment and selective relationships; Vhalzareth concentrates power and exploits enslaved labor. These are contested political responses, not instincts shared by all elves.
## Ilvaran
Faerûn's sun and moon elves both inform this high elven kingdom. Noble prestige follows lineage, learning, magical patronage and stewardship as well as property. Traveling households and foreign experience offer competing traditions. Existing monarchy and institutional foundations remain established.
Physical maturity occurs at roughly human speed; social adulthood is typically claimed around a century with an adult name. Young elves work, travel and form relationships before full social standing. Broad education can precede successive careers, but maintaining mastery requires work and resources. Long apprenticeships, patronage, travel between teachers and presentations can nurture talent or entrench gatekeepers.
Art includes living compositions altered over decades, answering performances responding to older works, and useful gifts demonstrating attentive understanding. Beauty includes well-made ordinary objects, gardens and infrastructure. Ilvaran excels at prepared defense, specialist magic, precision crafts and diplomacy, while replacements, concentrated privilege and simultaneous commitments strain it.
An exceptional inherited protective work was proposed as a cultural foundation; its form and capabilities remain open and cannot imply unlimited power. Corellon's hiding creates disagreements over preservation, self-government and outward cooperation.
Selective access protects inner sanctuaries. Overseas commitments are restricted; support for new households and expatriate return seeks continuity, while elite property and conditions on newcomers remain obstacles.
A mixed-settlement petition for a lightly inhabited border district has been debated for decades. Opposition extends beyond nobles and invokes lived memory, ancestral homes, graves, property, citizenship, representation, inheritance, sacred places and defense obligations. A thirty-year trial can consume a human family's adult life. Sponsorship or ancestry restrictions can produce hereditary subordination. No outcome is predetermined; exclusion and opening both carry costs.
## The Elderweald
Longstanding non-elven communities live under specific agreements. Familiar human villages can possess rights denied to unknown elves. Trust follows relationships rather than ancestry alone.
The monarchy coordinates external diplomacy, major defense and disputes but cannot unilaterally rewrite rights. Elven settlements maintain clearings, paths and defenders; non-elven villages hold residence, farming and gathering rights; mobile communities hold seasonal access; other intelligent forest inhabitants negotiate their own obligations.
Affected parties meet with a royal mediator. Records, markers and witnesses matter; elder memory is influential but fallible. Existing rights generally stand without agreement, giving beneficiaries of the status quo leverage. Temporary settlements can relieve needs.
Current dispute: a growing village seeks expanded farmland; neighboring elves defend old limits, a mobile community has seasonal use, and another intelligent inhabitant contests both ownership claims. Resolution remains open.
Communities inhabit managed forest, with ground-level homes, gardens, orchards, pasture, fisheries and seasonal sites. Elevated structures serve particular practical uses rather than housing everyone. Cutting wood is normal, but overlapping rights constrain particular trees.
Customs include the path round teaching routes and obligations, the first return sharing travel knowledge, winter visiting, and provisions for absent people on necessary service. Art includes answering songs, seasonal hangings and the Returning Green spring observance. Hunting prestige includes restraint, tracking and returning companions safely. Rillifane and Solonor's traditions influence religion; their arrival and present survival remain undecided. Priests and druids advise without automatically owning others' livelihoods.
## Vhalzareth society and government
Vhalzareth remains an oppressive Lolthite society. Great houses and senior priestesses control resources and people. Common free drow work in many occupations; free outsiders hold specific precarious protections. Enslaved people of multiple ancestries and their descendants are central to farming, domestic service, crafts, construction, administration and infrastructure.
Free common drow use patronage for housing, training, courts, dependents' care and protection. Patrons demand dues, service, information, loyalty and constraints on departure. Free status grants meaningful rights compared with slavery, but power determines enforcement. Guilds, neighborhoods and religious contacts offer limited alternatives houses seek to control.
Slavery does not erase family, cultural memory, skill or agency. Resistance includes flight, concealed resources, sabotage, protected knowledge and collective action under severe risks. Cultures of enslaved peoples have purposes beyond resisting their captors.
### Lolth and house conflict
Lolth tolerates intrigue and violence that reinforce dependence on her. Independent coalitions against her priesthood, threats to her sanctuary and damage jeopardizing essential infrastructure invite intervention. She is not omniscient: followers, surveillance, divine capabilities and attention supply imperfect knowledge. Priestesses manipulate religious accusations. Her need for competent cooperation conflicts with encouragement of rivalry.

| Veiled Sanctum | Public temples, schools and priestly residences; not Lolth's hidden physical sanctuary |

A shared market court supports recognized commerce. Water administration depends on specialists and competing appointments. Separate institutions train priestesses, military officers and arcane practitioners. A public time signal coordinates work underground. House interests cross districts rather than mapping neatly onto them.
Food, water, transport, fuel and labor underpin power. Farms need real nutrient inputs and imports; limited magic cannot create endless abundance. Houses use debts, contracts, supply restrictions and violence. Shared infrastructure imposes strategic constraints without protecting workers from abuse.
### Matrons and current dispute
| House | Matron | Position and objective |
| - | - | - |
| Vhal T'sarran | Zilvra Vhal T'sarran | Preeminent priestly house; seeks temple approval over senior shared appointments |
| Duskryn | Ilvara Duskryn | Food-estate challenger; seeks guaranteed water while concealing declining yields |
| Zauviir | Shyntlara Zauviir | Trade challenger; seeks an alternative route bypassing rival tolls and escorts |
| Velkynar | Drisinil Velkynar | Lower-ranked arcane house; seeks durable funded maintenance offices |
These are this setting's adopted houses and figures, not claims that they are all canonical Faerûn creations. Each has interests beyond its main specialty.
The Cisterns dispute concerns allocation, imports, repairs and religious oversight. Enslaved workers know details their masters lack. Exact conditions, concealed causes, veracity of specific claims and workers' responses remain open; Duskryn's declining-yield concern is adopted.
A Council of Matrons includes recognized noble houses beyond these four. Zilvra is First Matron, chairing common affairs and implementing agreed policy. Houses govern estates and clients; priesthood exercises cross-house religious authority. Lesser houses have seats under pressure. Common residents and enslaved people lack direct representation. Lolth can override this order. Council rulings require resources controlled by competing patrons.
### Culture dissent and outsiders
City beauty combines maintained illumination, towers, bridges, textiles, scent, metalwork, dance and distinctive piping, dissonant harmonies and keening voices. Prestige depends on often-uncredited enslaved work. Nedeirra is a competitive dance using harmless faerie-fire markers for missteps; our finite-reserve magic limits frequency and scale.
Family affection and friendship coexist with status demands and coercion. Noble education can advance relatives while enforcing hierarchy. Formal invitations, seating, gifts and attendance signal recognition and protection; common gatherings need not resemble elite receptions.
Local household customs include covered food for absent members, private humorous retellings, and repair gifts that can express trust or become debts.
Dissent includes rival religious traditions, escape networks, professional independence and limited reform. Opposition to one hierarchy does not guarantee opposition to slavery or domination. Eilistraee and Vhaeraun inspire traditions; their arrival and survival remain open.
Safe-conduct is issued by a house and registered at the Lantern Exchange, covering named visitors, purposes and routes for limited periods. Commerce depends on escorts, guarded quarters, intermediaries and consequences for violations. Foreign merchants may be protected while people of their homeland remain enslaved.
Manumission can be formally registered but contested; purchase, escape, collective resistance and foreign intervention provide other routes with serious limitations. Freedom and leaving the city are separate problems. Escape networks are small and relationship-based; enslaved people act for themselves rather than depending entirely on rescuers.
# Underdark neighboring societies
These five communities are original adopted settlements using Faerûn peoples. Their names and political situations belong to this setting.
| Community | Population and livelihood | Relationship and tension |
| - | - | - |
| Durnak Hold | Duergar foundries, iron workings and guarded caravan passage | Trades weapons and metalwork; competes over mines and captives and practices slavery. Merchants favor agreements while commanders seek route control |
| Hushvaults | Concealed deep gnome settlements, cultivation, stonework and mediated trade | Avoid dependence; some households shelter escapees, while others fear discovery |
| Sable Bloom | Myconid communities in damp caverns with managed decomposition and nutrient flows | Trades through intermediaries; disputes upstream pollution and whether to retreat or confront |
| Bellwater | Kuo-toa lake settlements, fisheries, ferries and shore markets | Useful crossings give leverage; religious leaders, boat owners and poorer fishers dispute dues and relations |
| Latch | Mixed goblin, kobold, quaggoth and other communities, including escaped captives, around an old supply fortress | Rejects ownership claims over residents. Recognition and discretion compete with helping those still enslaved |
None monopolizes all alternatives. Durnak offers convenient passage; Bellwater boats replace difficult travel around the lake. Goblins, kobolds and quaggoths also have communities beyond Latch. Grimlock and troglodyte settlements exist farther afield. Mixed towns and isolated dwarven holds broaden the region; illithids, beholders and aboleths remain available selectively, not fixed new neighbors.
Surrounding communities vary from enforceable asylum to forced return. Some trading enclaves recognize drow ownership claims; individual residents may defy them. Refuge, paid shelter, exploitative service conditions and religious aid all exist. Reaching a sympathetic polity can genuinely improve rights without ensuring total safety.
## Routes and survival geography
A main caravan passage links Vhalzareth to Durnak, with costly alternatives. Bellwater's lake connects multiple shores. Hushvaults use concealed side passages; Sable Bloom lies lower in a drainage system; Latch controls an exposed old junction. Possible links to Zauviir's route and the Cisterns crisis are not established causes.
Habitable pockets depend on food inputs, breathable air, water, waste handling, heat and maintained transport. Fungi and livestock require an ecological base; organic inputs come from waterways, animals, trade and managed waste, with exceptional magical ecosystems limited in scope.
Fissures and maintained ventilation support inhabited caverns; smoke, blockages and dangerous air close routes. Water rights and contamination cross borders. Drinking supplies and disposal must be separated; decomposition does not make every waste harmless. Furnaces need fuel and exhaust routes. Powerful communities can shift pollution and dangerous labor onto weaker ones.
The Convergence mismatched some drainage and ventilation systems. Centuries of adaptation managed many joins, but neglected works and changing seasonal flows can expose weaknesses. No specific major failure is fixed. Depth does not organize peoples into simple layers, and physically nearby settlements may be days apart by safe passages.
