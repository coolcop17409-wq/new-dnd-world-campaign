# Iron Dominion
## The Iron Dominion
The Iron Dominion is an orc-founded, multiethnic empire under living Gruumsh, its supreme ruler. The mortal emperor governs beneath him. All institutional authority is delegated by Gruumsh; captured divine spoils belong to him, with stewardship and rewards assigned at his discretion. Divided responsibilities create rivalry beneath him, not competing sovereignty. He sets priorities, issues commands, and intervenes selectively while the emperor directs government and conquest.
Founding orc clans retain advantages in senior appointments, land, and custodial privileges. Other subjects can advance through service and patronage. Conquered communities supply tribute, labor, recruits, and provisions; favored settler households expand imperial control through deliberate demographic displacement.
Expansion uses deliberate brutality: raids, hostage-taking, enslavement, deportation, confiscation, punitive destruction, collective punishment, and dependent rulers. Submission can preserve a community while leaving it exploited. Conquest rewards commanders and settlers but also creates insurgency, flight, corruption, and administrative strain.
Godslaying and fragment recovery serve conquest; they are not automatic features of every campaign. Gruumsh defeated and slew Maglubiyet during the Dominion's rise. Other victims, dates, methods, and the emperor's particular captured divine prize remain open. The Kheldorn frontier remains localized skirmishing, not total war.
### Emperor and delegated authority
The emperor is a Dominion-born orc, approximately seventy years old, who has ruled for roughly thirty years. Ordinary orcs average about fifty years. Substantial divine empowerment slows his aging and preserves his vigor; he remains killable. His ultimate lifespan and the consequences of losing that empowerment remain unresolved. Many early comrades have died, and their descendants now serve him.
He earned Gruumsh's appointment through conquest, capture of a major divine prize, and the ability to turn victory into sustained expansion. Military prestige, appointments, resource allocations, an imperial household, and a veteran following sustain his authority. He comes from a formerly secondary Stone Tusk household; his relatives have risen, while senior clan families resent his appointments across clan lines. His ambition is to make himself indispensable through institutions sustaining further conquest. Gruumsh can replace him.
The emperor's name remains unresolved: Vorgath and the epithet the Provisioner are proposals. His particular captured divine prize is unidentified.
Advancement rewards lasting additions to imperial power: territory held, populations subjected, divinity captured, and productive infrastructure secured. Useful subordinates can survive defeat; waste, cowardice, and defiance invite punishment.
| Authority beneath Gruumsh | Delegated responsibilities |
| - | - |
| Emperor and imperial administration | Campaigns, appointments, major granaries, military roads, settlement allocations, and supply distribution |
| Gruumsh's priesthood | Religious authority, particular sanctuaries, and divine expertise; priestly preferences do not automatically express Gruumsh's will |
| Founding clans | Estates, labor establishments, workshops, and revocable inherited custodial privileges |
| Specialist institutions | Maintenance, cultivation, healing, and knowledge required to use particular fragments; officials restrict their independence |
A sanctuary may have priestly custodians, imperial allocation of output, and clan-supplied labor. Rivalry produces bargaining, concealment, corruption, and bottlenecks without diminishing Gruumsh's supremacy.
### Divine infrastructure and brutal institutions
Accumulated divine power supports the Dominion's capacity to sustain expansion, including population growth, cultivation, endurance, healing, transport, and production. Its advantages come from beneficial empowerments administered through brutal institutions, rather than harmful side effects inherent to the blessings.
| Divine advantage | Imperial use and coercion |
| - | - |
| Greater fertility and healthier births | Forced reproductive programs only within reproductively compatible populations; cross-species reproduction is ordinarily impossible (the unique V species exception does not define Dominion populations). Children removed into state households, labor establishments or military upbringing. |
| Abundant and reliable harvests | Dispossession of conquered farmers, military settlement, slave estates, and requisitioned surpluses |
| Improved strength and endurance | Longer shifts and higher quotas imposed on enslaved workers |
| Rapid healing | Repeated return of soldiers to combat and injured laborers to dangerous work |
| Resilient roads and bridges | Forced relocation along supply corridors and clearance or abandonment of settlements outside the network |
| Exceptional animal breeding | Requisition and monopoly of breeding stock and transport animals |
| Productive furnaces and workshops | Seizure of skilled captives, family leverage, and guarded military establishments |
| Protection from disease | Preferential access for armies and favored settlements, distributed as a reward for obedience |
Military families, veterans, and favored towns receive real benefits. The regime directs those gains toward conquest, slavery, dispossession, and coercion. Specific sites, fragments, capabilities, and limits remain open; these effects do not apply universally across imperial territory. Accelerated maturation has not been established.
### Founding clan cultures
Several clans share six cultural strands; they are not exclusive occupations or six separate peoples.
| Strand | Cultural foundation and imperial transformation |
| - | - |
| Migrating hearth | Seasonal gathering grounds, portable household shrines, hospitality, and journey histories; borders restrict movement while elites acquire conquered pasture |
| Ancestral hold | Multigenerational compounds, communal stores, burial grounds, and ancestor names; victors establish prestigious new seats on confiscated land |
| Proven hand | Witnessed trials of craft, wrestling, animal handling, storytelling, and mastery; patronage rewards useful skill while elites seek exemptions for heirs |
| Sworn company | Adopted kin, shared ordeals, company feasts, and obligations to fallen companions' families; military loyalties cross clan boundaries |
| Keeping of names | Genealogies, mourning recitations, inherited objects, and disaster histories; imperial histories erase or distort inconvenient contributions |
| Sacred claim | Oaths, blessings, and Gruumsh's promise of a secure future through strength; the regime presents expansion as fulfillment |
Strength has multiple cultural meanings: courage, endurance, household survival, successful childbirth, and patient mastery. Imperial ideology directs them toward strengthening the Dominion.
Courtship includes negotiated obligations, romance, elopement, and disputes over children's household affiliation. Regional food and hospitality matter to reputation. Boast poetry, mocking songs, percussion, carving, decorated harness, and textiles sustain art and entertainment. Elders hold valued memories; empowered leaders can retain offices beyond ordinary lifespans. Compensation, witnesses, reconciliation, and adoption settle disputes, subject to imperial interference.
Clan assemblies allow recognized households to witness succession, settle disputes, and invoke leaders' obligations, though wealth dominates proceedings. Households provide illness support, burial, apprenticeship, and representation in exchange for labor, loyalty, and assistance. Marriage and adoption establish belonging and negotiated duties. Chiefs distribute resources and negotiate with officials; members can publicly demand promised support. Imperial privileges reward chiefs who deliver recruits, supplies, and compliance. Gruumsh can override every arrangement.
Clans contain great landholders, ordinary herders, artisans, indebted households, and landless veterans. Common ancestry does not imply equal benefit from conquest.
| Founding clan | Homeland and founding contribution | Present position and conflict |
| - | - | - |
| Stone Tusk | Upland valleys, fortified villages, pasture, and terraces; supplied gathering places and winter stores during early consolidation | Senior families hold estates, fortified settlements, and household troops; poorer members and veterans contest relatives' preference in land grants |
| Red Boar | River plains where settled communities and mobile herders met at seasonal markets; assembled animals, transport, and provisions | Leading branches hold herds, landings, caravan interests, and empowered breeding estates; mobile families resist imposed settlement and recruitment |
| Ashen Hand | Wooded foothills with holds, mines, farms, and furnaces; sustained equipment repair and captured strongholds | Leading households control military workshops with free and enslaved workers; independent masters resist absorption and appropriation of techniques |
Stone Tusk households preserve hearthstones and recount their histories at marriages and adoptions. Red Boar feasts publicly acknowledge debts of gratitude, making omissions consequential. Ashen Hand apprentices present both a finished object and a failed attempt; captive contributors can be denied credit. Each clan contains varied livelihoods and members living far from its homeland. These are three founding clans, not an exhaustive list.
### Conquered populations and military society
| Status | Position |
| - | - |
| Enslaved captives | Assigned or sold into estates, workshops, households, and state establishments; vulnerable to family separation |
| Tributary communities | Retain leaders and some customs under demands for supplies, labor, recruits, and hostages |
| Directly governed subjects | Imperial officials, taxation, movement controls, and unequal access to courts |
| Favored collaborators | Protection, offices, or property in exchange for enforcing imperial rule |
| Military settlers | Conquered land granted through service to founding-clan members and selected recruits from subjected peoples |
Orcs can be conquered; non-orcs can rise through usefulness, loyalty, wealth, and patronage. Selected households gain admission through service, sponsorship, marriage, or clan adoption; their descendants may become settlers themselves. Assimilation guarantees neither acceptance nor protection. Statuses can be revoked, and founding clans retain inherited advantages.
Marriage and adoption grant social and legal affiliation. Assimilated households and their descendants participate while retaining their species, subject to ordinary cross-species reproduction prohibitions; the singular extinct V-species exception in World Foundations does not generalize to them.
Early surrender can preserve local authority under tribute, hostages, and inspection. Strategic land invites direct rule and confiscation. Specialist communities may be preserved under guard and compulsory service. Prolonged resistance brings executions, enslavement, dispersal, and property seizure; rebellion can bring collective punishment and revoked privileges. These are tendencies shaped by imperial interests and Gruumsh's commands. Selected agreements are honored to encourage future surrender.
| Military element | Social and operational role |
| - | - |
| Imperial core | Standing formations supported by empowered infrastructure; pay, prestige, and potential settlement grants |
| Clan contingents | Obligated forces retaining leaders and loyalties beneath imperial command |
| Subject auxiliaries | Recruits with local expertise; service can earn particular privileges without general equality |
| Garrisons | Hold crossings, estates, depots, and occupied towns; enforce imperial rule |
| Coerced labor columns | Build camps, move supplies, and maintain routes under guard, excluded from soldiers' rewards |
Military households follow postings, establish businesses, raise children, and petition for benefits. Settlements can grow around generations of service.
### Settlement and demographic displacement
Conquest deliberately transforms populations as well as government. Faster reproduction among favored orc and goblinoid populations, reinforced by divine support and preferential resources, drives settlement and displacement. Peoples need not share identical rates; exact fertility, maturation, and population figures remain unspecified.
Garrisons and military households establish footholds; confiscation, enslavement, relocation, and land grants expand settlement. Subsequent generations seek more holdings and increasingly dominate language, religion, administration, and inheritance. Successful long-term occupation tends toward demographic replacement. Conquered peoples persist as minorities, displaced communities, enslaved populations, and diasporas.
Governors are assessed partly on how many settler households their provinces can sustain. Surveys, veteran grants, empowered cultivation, and reproductive institutions serve this policy. Surrender terms may preserve immediate survival without securing future access to land, water, influence, or room for descendants. Confiscation, coerced sale, and relocation continue displacement during peace.
| Broad zone | Character |
| - | - |
| Old heartland | Clan territories and early conquests where past displacement has largely become accepted imperial history |
| Settled provinces | Growing settler majorities or substantial settler populations alongside subjected communities |
| Conquest frontier | Garrisons, new settlements, contested grants, displacement, and active resistance |
These describe stages, not fixed administrative boundaries. After generations, military liberation cannot simply restore an earlier population or society: many descendants of settlers were born locally.
### Goblinoids and Maglubiyet
Goblins have numerous farming households, towns, workshops, settler communities, and soldiers; their contribution exceeds the recognition founding orc clans grant them. Hobgoblins retain military traditions, provincial governments, and officer households seeking authority matching their responsibilities. Bugbears have dispersed settlements and extended kin groups with varied livelihoods; settlement and service demands can conflict with autonomy. All include privileged beneficiaries, ordinary subjects, and enslaved captives.
Gruumsh's defeat and Godslaying of Maglubiyet was a foundational victory during the Dominion's rise. Captured power supports military command and coordination; its distribution and exact capabilities remain open. Submitting leaders retained selected commands and local authority; opponents faced death, displacement, or enslavement. Imperial doctrine claims the victory entitles Gruumsh to his former followers' allegiance.
Religious responses include sincere acceptance of Gruumsh, continued devotion to the dead god and interest in surviving fragments, revival of older traditions, and new faiths. Some remember Maglubiyet as a conqueror whose death offered freedom. His ordinary clerics lost divine spellcasting under the world's established rules. The survival and whereabouts of older goblinoid gods remain open.
Independent goblinoids remain in scattered villages, clan territories, hidden settlements, and mixed communities beyond the Dominion. Local leadership and temporary cooperation exist, but no overarching goblinoid state, league, or regional power is established for the political map. Maglubiyet's death did not transfer their allegiance.
### Religious rule and limits of expansion
Household rites and ancestral observances are generally permitted unless associated with resistance. Submitting temples may operate under tribute, inspection, and leadership restrictions while losing land, schools, and public freedom. Divine sanctuaries are claimed for Gruumsh; custodians may be compelled to maintain them. Clergy organizing resistance or sheltering fugitives face arrests, confiscation, and punishment. Surviving gods in conquered territory require direct submission, expulsion, capture, or Godslaying. Older orc traditions can persist beneath Gruumsh; other orc gods' survival, including Luthic's, remains open.
Distance still requires transport, depots, and escorts. Occupation consumes troops, officials, collaborators, and labor. Clans, soldiers, temples, and governors compete for rewards. Local terrain and hostile populations impede campaigns, and captured sites require specialist knowledge. Consolidation makes conquests productive; further attacks promise resources to satisfy supporters. Gruumsh chooses, while subordinates compete to influence him and allocate blame.
### An unnamed recently conquered province
A provincial field army was defeated; several towns negotiated surrender, while an upland fortress still resists. Imperial authorities seek harvests, depots, and a route for another campaign. Veterans were promised land protected by surrender terms. A captured agricultural sanctuary remains productive, but its existing custodians hold expertise the occupiers cannot readily replace.
The governor prioritizes production, a clan commander demands rewards, and Gruumsh's priests oversee the sanctuary. Local leaders variously cooperate to preserve communities, profit from confiscations, or support resistance. The immediate conflict is whether to honor surrender terms when they delay military settlement. Outcomes remain open.
The province's name, peoples, former allegiance, and location are unresolved. It is not established as Kheldorn or any other named neighbor.
