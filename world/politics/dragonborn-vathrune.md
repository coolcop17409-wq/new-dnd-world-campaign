# Vathrune and the dragonborn compact
Vathrune is an independent pilgrimage state on northern Namarra. The Vathrune Compact joins it with the five dispersed custodial city-states and represented communities; shared liberation traditions predate the Convergence.
Vathrune preserves Tiamat's tomb and shared ceremonial grounds. Ashcrown's liberated stronghold arose at Varkhaz's fortified seat, not a common national capital. Crownscar is a superseded country name.
Households form houses, extended lineages or sworn associations. Multiple houses form each great custodial clan. Ashcrown, Stormhold, Thornveil, Blackweir, and Frostbound are independent city-states, each governed by its established houses and responsible for one captive Great Wyrm. Their territory includes supporting farms, villages, harbors, and defensive approaches. Leading houses depend on supporting households and communities rather than governing an undifferentiated clan population.
Dragons from the same original world arrived alongside the dragonborn. Some surviving dragons may personally remember the struggle for independence. Not every dragon is an enemy, but dragonborn sovereignty and distrust of dragon domination are central.
Custodial city-states are dispersed: Frostbound in northwestern Arden; Ashcrown on Arden's southern peninsula; Blackweir in the Broken Isles; Stormhold offshore southeastern Orath; Thornveil on southwestern Namarra. Sea voyages can delay aid and inspections for weeks. Vathrune is outside either empire's immediate frontier.
## Captivity and fivefold symbolism
Before the Convergence, the five Great Wyrms slew Tiamat. Their injuries enabled separate dragonborn captures. Motives and degree of cooperation remain open. Her tomb at Vathrune attracts pilgrims; death ended her ordinary clergy's spells. Burial does not establish where all her fragments went.
Captive Great Wyrms remain intelligent adversaries, not obedient mounts or reliable battlefield weapons. Confinement combines physical works, guards, finite magical protection, bargains, and knowledge of individual behavior. Threatened release is catastrophic and uncertain.
Vathrune is also the invented ancestral name of a vessel holding five flames. Its emblem is a five-branched ceremonial brazier with no flame at the center. Officially the flames express unity; custodians see constant maintenance; dissenters see shared institutions sustained by five imprisoned lives. The five colors and flames also recall Tiamat and the shared aftermath of her death. Five-wick household lamps express belonging and remembrance without necessarily endorsing captivity.
Farms, workshops, roads, and ordinary commerce sustain the population. Captivity consumes resources and legitimizes privilege; it does not create abundant magical industry.
## Draconic deterrence and strategic anxiety
Great Wyrms are strategic-scale threats: a dragon's flight, breath, cunning, and longevity can change what armies, rulers, and fortified cities dare risk. Political leaders consider attacks on lairs, reprisals, threatened release, border evacuations, treasury dispersal, hidden stores, alliances, warning systems, and the consequences of a mistaken accusation. Rumors of a dragon's movement can influence shipping, diplomacy, insurance, troop deployment and popular fear even when no attack occurs. Independent intelligent dragons have their own claims, memories, bargains and interests; they are not automatically allied to any state.
The five captive Great Wyrms are existential last-resort deterrents for their individual dragonborn custodial city-states, grounded in self-preservation, not routine coercion or expansion. Their continuing captivity and the feared consequences of an attempted release can deter an enemy from trying to destroy or conquer a custodial state. Ordinary diplomatic disputes, commerce, status rivalries, raids, territorial bargaining and political theater do not justify credible explicit release threats. The custodians should issue such threats very rarely, if at all, and only in response to an imminent, extreme threat to their people's survival or sovereignty. A release is an uncertain, potentially suicidal act: the intelligent Great Wyrm is NOT controlled, cannot be ordered to attack an adversary or spare allies, and may first destroy its captors or nearby civilians. Possessing a captive is not equivalent to commanding it, targeting a weapon, guaranteeing retaliation, or delivering a dependable military strike. This is strategic deterrence by catastrophic risk, not a deployable arsenal or a standing policy of mutual assured destruction. The five states retain distinct custody, politics and decisions; an existential threat to one does not automatically entitle another to release its captive. Diplomatic leverage ordinarily comes from the reputation and risk, without announcing willingness to unleash a Great Wyrm. Do not escalate casual rivalries to dragon threats; weigh rescue, concealment, withdrawal, coalition defense and preservation of the population first. Independent dragons remain autonomous powers, and lesser riding wyrms are not strategic weapons. Outsiders may wrongly assume dragonborn control their prisoners, despite their liberation history.

## Shared compact institutions
The compact coordinates delegated diplomacy, defense, pilgrimage, markets, arbitration, and aid. Its Vathrune headquarters has no authority over that state's domestic government.
The compact prohibits directing a release toward another member's territory, requires immediate reporting of confinement failure, and forbids pledging the compact collectively to a dragon or foreign power. Members may not seize Tiamat's tomb or claim exclusive possession of her remains.
| Institution | Authority and limits |
| - | - |
| Assembly | Custodial city-states, chartered communities and non-clan delegations have representation with unequal influence; approves major expenditure, common obligations, foreign commitments, sanctions and extraordinary levies |
| Vanquisher | Fixed-term elected coordinator of defense and diplomacy; modest common force protects pilgrims, envoys and agreed routes; cannot routinely police dispersed states |
| Arbitration panels | Particular disputes heard by uninvolved territories |
| Custodial specialists | Commissioned for particular inquiries; expertise does not confer sovereign authority |
Compact-authorized inspections require specific evidence and support from multiple uninvolved members. Access and travel must be arranged with the host state; authorization does not grant instant entry. Confidentiality protects unrelated secrets but cannot excuse failure to demonstrate safety. Distance, local obstruction, and manipulation limit oversight.
Enforcement escalates through arbitration, compensation or sureties, restrictions on privileges and pressure on leading houses, then coalition intervention as a last resort. Essential confinement supplies should not be casually withheld; supervised delivery can protect communities without rewarding leaders.
## Elections and compact balance
Vanquisher candidates need nomination by two great clans and at least one chartered community. Election requires four of five clan delegations and a majority of non-clan delegations. Service lasts six years without immediate reelection. The winner relinquishes direct custodial command and major clan offices. Offensive campaigns and extraordinary levies need assembly approval.
Towns retain local taxation, markets, militia, and specified road rights. Lesser houses influence clan delegations, alliances, and compact offices. Neither the five great clans nor non-clan communities can elect the Vanquisher alone.
Ashcrown relies on prestige but bears costly custody; Stormhold offers guarded bargains but faces suspicion; Thornveil offers outside information but invites questions about manipulation; Blackweir has valuable expertise but high costs; Frostbound has defensive terrain but difficult supplies. Coalitions shift by issue rather than forming permanent blocs.
A captive's death or escape creates contested questions over custody-based privileges, territory, compensation and representation. No universally settled replacement procedure exists. Capturing another is never routine.
## Cultural foundations and open details
Cultural traditions include chosen kinship and fosterage, witnessed clan membership and departure, adulthood through civilian or military responsibility, liberation-era objections to pledging persons for debt, and scrutiny of agreements with dragons. Antiphonal songs, recitation, patterned cloth and ceramics, shaded courts and valley/plateau regional variation support everyday life. The Day of Open Gates commemorates liberation. Whether institutions honor those ideals is contested.
The central contradiction is freedom secured partly through enslaving former masters. Dragonborn independence does not imply that their rulers reject slavery consistently.
City-state borders, population, rulers, precise confinement works, bargains, and custodial disputes remain open, as does Vathrune's resident representation in shared institutions. The five Great Wyrms are exceptional captives; other dragons remain independent beings.
## Vathrune government and lesser wyrms
A resident civic council governs Vathrune's residents, markets, harbors, public works, and ordinary law. Sworn tomb custodians protect the burial precinct, maintain rites, and regulate access to Tiamat's remains. The compact assembly handles delegated common affairs; the Vanquisher cannot command Vathrune's domestic government. Worshippers, mourners, and people honoring dragonborn history visit without all necessarily worshipping Tiamat. Custodians' actual access to divine remnants remains unresolved.
Dragonborn maintain a small number of lesser wyrms bred and trained for flight. These are draconic animals with strong memory and individual temperaments, distinct from the captive Great Wyrms. They lack humanoid-level speech, divine power, and devastating chromatic breath weapons. Slow breeding, demanding diets, and scarce suitable nesting grounds limit numbers.
A lesser wyrm usually carries one rider and light equipment. Riders require years of training and familiarity; changing riders is possible but difficult. Specialist households and establishments maintain them, and ownership does not automatically confer riding rights. They support reconnaissance, messages, and rare personal transport, constrained by weather, rest, landing sites, and vulnerability to weapons, magic, and larger creatures. Sea routes remain essential for pilgrims, troops, food, and commerce.
