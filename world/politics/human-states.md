# The ten major human states
Four native states survived the Convergence, one human state arrived from another world, and five were founded afterward. Other minor human polities also exist. These counts describe historical and predominant populations, not ancestry restrictions on citizenship.
| State | Origin | Political foundation |
| - | - | - |
| Kingdom of Ossent | Native survivor | Hereditary crown constrained by nobles, chartered cities, and inherited rights |
| Kingdom of Zafira | Native survivor | River monarchy dependent on provincial landowners, agriculture, irrigation, and navigation |
| Kingdom of Talassar | Native survivor | Maritime monarchy with territory on Arden and Namarra; fleet depends on powerful port cities |
| Drusk Confederacy | Native survivor | Highland rulers retain local authority and choose common leadership through an assembly |
| Veyran Commonwealth | Transplanted | Bane's former kingdom; totalitarian military state led by First Marshal Ilyra Vask |
| Crownsunder | New | Breakaway kingdom founded through a disputed succession and war with Ossent |
| The Charter Coast | New | Merchant republic of ports on Arden and Namarra, independent of Talassar |
| Oathmark | New | Frontier federation formed from settlements claimed and taxed by Zafira but inadequately defended |
| Lastlight | New | Founded by survivors after Lathander's death in battle, approximately 300 years ago |
| Allhaven | New | Predominantly human, mixed-origin and multiethnic state founded through a negotiated charter at a trade junction |
## Regional histories and cultures
### Ossent and Crownsunder
Crownsunder separated about 80 years ago. The monarch outlived an eldest son, whose young daughter was the hereditary heir. The monarch's younger brother produced a deathbed testament naming himself successor, citing foreign influence through the granddaughter's marriage agreement. He secured council recognition and coronation in the capital. Her supporters initially fought for the whole kingdom; stalemate produced Crownsunder. Both present dynasties descend from these claimants. The testament's authenticity and the monarch's authority to alter succession remain unresolved; no god settled the matter. War duration, later campaigns and armistice date remain open.
Ossent emphasizes continuity through recognized institutions; Crownsunder emphasizes fidelity to hereditary rights and sworn obligations. Both value law, family and promises. Confiscated estates, offices and privileges give elites material interests in their version of events. The border divides a preexisting cultural region, and some villages maintain memorials to people who fought on both sides. Ossent does not recognize Crownsunder's independence; Crownsunder's ruler claims Ossent's throne. Their armistice permits restricted trade across a militarized frontier marked by raids, disputed settlements, and support for exiles.

Regional livelihoods cross borders: grain, orchards and timber-framed market towns in the heartland; sheep, hardy crops, wool and stone buildings in uplands; fisheries, mills and barges along rivers. Courts display status through imports and ceremony. Older household rites coexist with Chauntea, Tyr, other faiths and Lathander's surviving communities. Neither crown has unified religious endorsement.
Ossent retained Orsaven, the old capital, with its treasury, archives and royal offices and is more centralized. Crownsunder depends on noble and town support: its Articles of Recognition require each monarch to renew guarantees concerning taxation, jurisdiction and obligations. The assembly can defend local rights while entrenching elite privilege. Ossent's royal courts can curb lords or extend abuse.
Western Ossent river governance (D199-SECONDARY-CHANNEL-CANON-001, adopted Day199): Orsaven's active main commercial river access is subject to Crown and local institutional protection, NOT a decades-long vacant route. On the less profitable older secondary distributary, maintenance and permanent coverage had diminished without extinguishing agriculture, fisheries or local barge traffic; the Compact's armed seizure of Miregate/Reedbed/old riverworks was comparatively recent. The finite Compact occupied vulnerable works and threatened lowland settlements; it did not hold the Crown's principal shipping river or major cities. V's intervention preceded full conventional resolution. Exact historic Crown investigations, ordered troops, occupation dates and how/when officials learned the full flooding scheme remain unplayed/undefined, so do not assert specifics. Any future Crown hereditary grant/warden commission preserves public rights and Crown hydraulic control, is not a river toll monopoly.
In Orsaven’s western freight district, the Exchange can pay for evidence about a merchant’s losses. At Harrowgate Yard the city watch detained suspects and took custody of the goods. Bellmont Trading House supplied the inventory used to identify its property. Merchants, watch officers and owners each had a different part in the recovery. Exchange Hall also provides commercial introductions and named invitations to the Lowland Contracts Reception: carriers, underwriters and buyers negotiate independently, and an invitation or introduction creates no trade contract or political alliance.
Orsaven's western silk market includes workshop traffic, lower workers' lodgings, a public stair under Watch authority and a separate privately owned covered arcade. The arcade is a useful detour rather than the only route. Claims about the recent stair assaults, private guards and toll proceeds remain an unresolved local investigation; the Continuity Pack holds earned clues and private preparation separately.
At Three Bells Square, House Belgrave exhibited the Harrowfield Saber under a royal postwar grant. Its woven-band pattern also identified it to a grieving Rethwyn of Crownsunder. In the Old Crown Ward, the lender kept custody while the family held a private remembrance. The arrangement let the family honor its dead and Belgrave borrow against the blade for household pensions. The families still differ on its provenance.
Against Veyr, Ossent conditions cooperation on suspension of Crownsunder's throne claim. Crownsunder seeks aid without that concession; its assembly divides over expenditure and accommodation. Border communities want fewer troops facing each other, but land claims make withdrawals risky. Rulers' names remain open.
### Orsaven and Caereth — lived districts and social circles
Adopted local social texture (ADMIN-CULTURE-PASS-20261118-01), grounded in the established Ossent/Crownsunder regional history. These patterns guide ordinary residents and scene dressing, not exact new district boundaries, hidden discoveries, named NPCs or a changed map. Both cities are populated, commercially active and accustomed to mixed-species residents; individual prejudices exist but open hostility is not a citywide norm. Social class, work, native/immigrant upbringing, local ties and personal convictions can make neighbors disagree about the same issue. Their differences should affect negotiations, shelter, trust and ordinary choices without turning every conversation into a political lecture.
Orsaven — old royal capital of Ossent:
- Royal and Old Crown civic circles: royal offices, old records, inherited titles and conflicting postwar provenance make precise witness language, institutional authority and household reputation consequential. A clerk may insist on evidence; a noble patron may prize a family symbol; either can be courteous, protective, self-interested or wrong. The established Three Bells Square / Old Crown Ward Harrowfield Saber remembrance shows an Ossent grant and Crownsunder family memory coexisting without settled agreement on ownership.
- Western freight and Exchange households: loading crews, carriers, brokers, underwriters, shopowners and watch officers treat dependable deliveries, verified ownership, street safety and referrals as daily concerns. The Exchange and Harrowgate Yard have already been used in played freight cases; do not restart them or imply their watch custody changed. A seasoned carter and a royal fiscal officer can cooperate while speaking in very different registers. The city's functioning main commercial river access is protected and active; it is NOT the diminished Miregate secondary branch or the Dornal.
- Western silk market: workshops, lower worker lodgings, public stairs and a separately owned covered arcade already exist. Street users, private owners, artisans and city watch have different access interests. An artisan may joke about the price of thread; a resident may remember an actual stair hazard; a private-property claim is not a universal curfew. The prior investigation and its evidence remain whatever the live records establish.
- Hospices, religious and learning circles: existing Keepers of the Last Road, Saint Orlan's and learned contacts have different reasons to value names, evidence, funerary care or education. A Keeper speaking for the dead need not speak like a royal judge; someone harmed by the Quenwood case can distrust a particular endowment without assuming all clerics are corrupt. These circles and their networks do not create a new district, combined conspiracy or blanket religious institution.
- Mixed neighborhoods and commercial visitors: locals of different species, long-settled arrivals and travelers can share Orsaven mannerisms without the same politics. Workers may borrow a neighbor's language or oath; a high elf raised here can sound like an Orsaven merchant, and an orc citizen can oppose the Dominion. These are social possibilities, not introduced NPCs or automatic rights. Most city inhabitants chiefly care about work, households and local routes, not constant argument over Crownsunder.
Caereth — fortified capital of Crownsunder:
- Court and household retainers: reputation, recognized hereditary claims, witnessed oaths and the Articles of Recognition can influence how nobles and their stewards make promises. A servant or commoner may have practical obligations but need not support a patron's politics. Shared Tyr and Chauntea rites remain plausible, not exclusive.
- Fortified-road and watch communities: veterans, smiths, drivers, small shopkeepers and families near eastern approaches may think in terms of road security, patrol needs and household protection. They distinguish actual Crown orders from rumor. Veyr's expansion on Orath is geographically more salient than the distant Iron Dominion campaign on Arden, but an imminent attack on Caereth is not established.
- Markets, stableyards and crafts: livestock, cart supply, cloth, food, quarry stone and trade contacts produce brisk bargaining and ordinary mutual dependence. A merchant who deals with Ossent may recognize a customer's accent, joke about “which Crown,” then conduct business normally. An immigrant merchant may share Caereth obligations without inheriting every dynasty preference.
- Households experiencing bereavement: traditional woven bands, modest memorials, Long Vigil and guest-protection obligations can produce differing decisions about who is welcomed, invited or commemorated. Lady Elara's current city house and her separate Rethwyn rural manor remain distinct places with separate people and knowledge. Nothing here delivers news from the manor to Caereth or commits Elara to a rite.
- Surrounding countryside and rural Rethwyn: pasture, woodland rights, older service roads, farming, shepherding, weather and local household ties make speech more direct and seasonal than court style. Acting keeper Sela Durn has independent rural priorities and specific present knowledge; do not give her unplayed answers, add new tenants or introduce a pre-Convergence ruin in the cellar by assumption.
Shared and contested identity: Orsaven and Caereth both inherit a pre-split cultural region; the succession war is only about eighty years old, while the Convergence is around 1,200 years old. Families can retain relationships, hospitality and trade across the frontier while governments disagree about legitimacy. Someone may mourn relatives who fought opposite each other, or see a foreign merchant as a reliable partner. Strong hatred requires an earned personal, historical or institutional basis; ordinary urban conversation need not repeatedly discuss dynasties.
Visible history: old native masonry beside later repairs, ancestral road alignments, inherited inscriptions, household memorial cloth and reused foundations can provide plausible physical atmosphere. No new named street, monument, Convergence anomaly, underground passage, mapped crossing or building age is thereby fixed; existing place and river facts retain priority. Select specific dressings when actually established and record durable material in the appropriate world/atlas owner. Orsaven's Old Crown and commercial quarters may look different without every wall being mysterious.
Deployment: On entering an established circle, choose one locally observable social habit, one tangible trade/faith/history detail and one plausible attitude toward a neighbor; then let the speaker's individual motive lead. Do not apply every detail to every NPC, assume uniform citizenship, force a quest hook, or overwrite played contacts or district geometry.
### Zafira and Oathmark
Oathmark separated about 220 years ago. Zafira recognizes its government while contesting practical terms. Oathmark controls important headwaters; Zafira controls useful downstream markets; Oathmark also has its own southern coast and direct overseas access. Seasonal delegations use records and witnesses, but neither trusts a permanent common authority. Disputes cover diversions, reservoirs, timber floating, flood warnings, navigation dues and defense contributions. Zafira benefits from Oathmark's frontier defense while disputing borders and tributary rights.

Lower-valley farming, orchards, fish and flood-adapted buildings contrast with upland livestock, hardy crops, timber and stone across national borders. Zafira prizes basin-wide royal coordination; Oathmark emphasizes authority earned through work and protection.
Oathmark assemblies primarily represent recognized households, including female-headed households. Tenants, apprentices, itinerant workers and newcomers can contribute without independent representation. Zafiran village councils coexist with large estates and canal associations whose influence follows established land and water entitlements. Royal river courts offer costly, politically uneven appeals.
### Talassar and the Charter Coast
Charter Coast independence occurred about 160 years ago after extraordinary naval taxes and restrictions on foreign trade. Merchant councils led, but sailors, shipwrights, dockworkers and small traders supplied labor and fighting. Benefits were unequal. Talassar recognizes independence under a treaty imposing compensation and naval concessions. Heavy trade continues alongside competition over shipping and markets; the Coast seeks revisions while Talassar insists the obligations remain binding.
Port councils favor commercial households and influential guilds. An Assembly of Harbors coordinates common tariffs, diplomacy, naval contributions and treaty obligations. An elected admiral depends on member ships and funding. Crew and guild associations bargain without equivalent political representation. Talassar's crown can restrain local elites while demanding taxes and service.

Maritime religion includes appeasement of Umberlee, Selûne's navigation traditions, drowned ancestors, remembered rescuers and negotiated crew observances. Pilots, rescue boats, signal fires and storm shelters remain essential independent institutions. Umberlee and Selûne's current status remains open for PC creation.
### Drusk and Allhaven
Drusk values household reputation, kinship, residence and fulfilled obligations. Guests often require someone to answer for their conduct. Allhaven bases citizenship on residence and civic obligations, with public accommodations and charter courts serving mixed communities. Its shared assembly handles common affairs while founding communities retain local rights and protected representation. Drusk's local rulers control passes, tolls, and roads, complicating confederate agreements.
Shared customs include public winter-store accounting, protected market routes called road peace, returning travelers' gifts or news, and witness tokens supporting agreements across language and literacy. Drusk includes cosmopolitan pass towns as well as remote valleys; adopted newcomers can gain belonging.
Allhaven was founded about 500 years ago. Founding wards and incorporated communities retain guaranteed representation despite population shifts. Newer districts pay taxes and provide service without equal influence. Reform must address entrenched privilege without destroying smaller communities' protections. These divisions cross ancestry.
Drusk elects a First Speaker for seven years through delegations selected by member valleys, lordships and chartered towns. The office coordinates diplomacy, arbitration, agreed defenses and contributions. New levies, offensive war, extraordinary taxes and permanent foreign garrisons require member consent. Contributions vary between money, troops, guides and supplies. Home-territory ties invite scrutiny. The current officeholder fears Veyr but struggles to secure commitments. Warden titles are reserved for the Wardens of Tyr.
## Lastlight
Lathander died in battle before Lastlight existed. Survivors—including soldiers, displaced civilians, and former clergy—gathered around his final sanctuary, the place where his light last shone. Their refuge became a sovereign state governed through a compact among communities, military defenders, and temple custodians.
Lastlight was founded approximately 300 years ago after Lathander's death ended his ordinary clergy's spells. Its compact's exact offices, the battlefield enemy, and circumstances remain open; Lastlight did not kill him.
Lastlight's culture emphasizes practical renewal, truthful remembrance and refuge. Teaching, care and rebuilding sustain Lathander's ideals. Whether a 300-year-old refuge owes shelter to present strangers is contested.
Talassar is Lastlight's nearest major mainland trading partner. The Charter Coast offers competing shipping and import connections; the Accord remains a valued but more distant partner. Ashcrown, Thornveil, and Oathmark are also trade partners. Oathmark ships directly from its southern coast rather than relying on foreign ports. Many arrivals pass through Talassari or Charter Coast ports, making maritime relations important to access to refuge. Trade does not imply military alliance or endorsement of domestic practices. Specific additional goods exchanged with Ashcrown, Thornveil, and Oathmark remain proposals.

Protected guests receive emergency shelter and food without compulsory work. Residents may work, rent, form households and use courts. Recognized members gain participation and communal rights, ordinarily after three years' residence and civic obligations. No conversion, military service or wealthy sponsor is required; exceptions cover children, reunification and inability to serve. Recognition may rely on testimony rather than documents. Local obstruction and limited resources challenge the promises.
