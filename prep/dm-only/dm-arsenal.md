The hour before the arsenal opens • An urban siege in three scenes
Latest pacing: this remains an optional local situation. Focus play on access, protecting people and defeating armed theft rather than mandatory pension bookkeeping. Protest, theft or possession of a weapon alone does not establish a lethal threat. Preserve the porters' flight and the civic dispute; do not turn everyone murderous to satisfy the encounter preference. Resolve any actual deadly attack and companion reactions under RULES. The royal pay hall occupies the front of an old city arsenal. V could hear pensioners singing its work song from the market or meet a drummer carrying broth through the cordon. Eleven retired guards and dependents have taken the hall after a third missed payment. The paymaster holds the key to the grated passage outside the store. The front crowd blocks the normal approach. Their spokeswoman, Janna Creel, says the paymaster stays with them until the money comes. A young armorer is inside by choice. Her father has heard she is a hostage. The royal arsenal’s escort captain needs access to the separate rear store by the next scheduled arms convoy. Delay costs her the escorts for its road stage.
At the front, several approaches put the party within speaking distance. The captain offers safe passage to a physician. Neighbors pass soup through an old proofing slit. From a fletcher’s loft V can see the well court between the pay hall and the rear store. The armorer can speak for herself through that slit. The paymaster can explain the missing money. Across three payment dates he spent the households’ three installments on wagons carrying threatened people to safety on Ossent soil, including relatives from Crownsunder. The journeys happened. He had no authority to charge the pensioners for them. Janna recognizes one of the wagoners and still wants her pay. Two pensioners block the gate if the paymaster tries to leave. He will surrender the key for a safe escort, which may break Janna’s hold over the negotiations.
At the rear, two pulls on the tally rope are the broker’s signal once the outer gate is left unguarded. Porters hired by an arms broker are rolling one crate of crossbows toward an unguarded hatch while the watch faces the hall. If V already secured the hatch, they leave the crate alone. A watcher can follow them back to the buyer instead. A wheel squeals and the armorers can say how many crates should be moving. Reach the rear store through the roof walk, escort royal guards across the court under an agreed truce, or let the crate go while extracting people from the front. Two porters will scatter if challenged. The broker’s armed minder wants the crate through the hatch. Show cover, bystanders and both exits before initiative.
Closure can be a protected armory, safe access, recovered arms or an agreed civil arrangement; pension accounting and later hearings can stay with the people responsible.
At the convoy bell, the captain must choose whether to send her escort on or keep it for a forced entry. V can broker a conversation with Janna at any earlier point, cover the arms transfer, or let the order run its course. The captain can delay one escort or secure the armory without storming the pay hall. The captain can guarantee safe passage. She has no pension cash. A noble guarantor can send a steward for one installment in return for public credit. The paymaster’s sealed voucher names the evacuees. A carter can attest to the journeys and amount without naming passengers. Publishing the voucher is stronger evidence at their expense. The pensioners might accept a first payment and a named guarantor or hold out for all three. Recovering the weapons, averting a forced entry, paying the families and protecting the evacuees are distinct victories. If the convoy bell sounds with the hatch clear, the thieves take the crate. If the front is still shut, she must weigh the armorer’s account, the presence of a physician and any truce actually reached. She may keep a cordon and send a smaller escort. A forced entry requires an immediate danger or an order she is willing to carry out. Once those outcomes occur, return to the infirmary, the pensioners’ relief supper or a search for a specific buyer. V may avert either or both by acting earlier. The paymaster’s decision has consequences even after the siege ends.
