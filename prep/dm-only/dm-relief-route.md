A crossing in small type • Hawkers, quays and the eastern wards
Dunwall boatwright Dena Voski recognizes her sister’s skiff in a hawker’s sketch of a relief landing. She puts her thumb over the notch in the painted bow. “Write about what they carried. Leave that bit out.” An Allhaven factor wants evidence before advancing money for another load. The news seller’s editor wants a named eyewitness because other sheets accuse the fund of inventing deliveries. The skiff still serves people in occupied territory. The sketch came by wagon and several hands, with a dated covering note.
Give the party things it can test. The return wagon carries empty numbered medicine chests with the receiving hospice’s distinctive repair to a broken lid. A drover went the whole way and recalls one chest arrived spoiled. A separately carried acknowledgment identifies that chest and quantity. Let them compare the two accounts separately or show the factor the matched return goods. Dena can demonstrate the unloading rig without identifying her family. A competing editor bids for the original sketch. Street runners will take the next edition to carriers’ yards, where agents buy news for eastern routes. A recognizable boat could then be reported to Veyran patrols. The date of that risk depends on real travel.
The Allhaven factor risks her firm’s reserve, and a Drusk carrier offers only the pack space his own valley entrusted to him. They will release the next shipment on independent verification. The party can seek this private advance, publish a corroborated account with a redrawn bow, or release the original. Give Dena and the wagoner a chance to object before their families’ exposure is decided. Holding everything back may cost the next load its credit. Once pages circulate, a fresh job is to warn the landing through a real commercial relay and find another willing carrier. Its people choose whether to move. News returns on route time. No city act liberates Dunwall or speaks for a unified resistance.
