Border and countryside options
Status and scope
Three independent options: a dangerous animal hunt, a civic warning project, and lower-priority stock recovery. None is a mandatory capture job. RULES governs actual threats and consequences; petty theft need not become the main adventure pipeline.
LEVEL7 REVIEW — ADMIN-LEVEL7-PREP-20261118-001. Keep the animal problem short and locally meaningful; do not breed extra giant boars or convert stock thieves into elite soldiers to match advancement. Improved observation can read the existing lane/hedge signs; trained Athletics and a later chosen Fenar movement adaptation can make hurdles, banks and rescue routes easier. These are fieldwork options, not mandatory checks on every fence. Existing stakes and payments do not inflate with level. Only a selected expedition needs its exact creature count/profile, departure route and terms fixed before clues or rolls; other options remain light reserves. Bells can remain civic play with no combat.

A warning both hills know • Palace workshops and the palace-side bridge
A bellfounder strikes a bronze cup the size of her palm. On the far bank a second cup answers, then a larger bell. Two Orseld villages on opposite sides of the disputed frontier have asked for a civilian warning signal when armed horsemen drive stock off the shared uplands. Someone has paid for the metal. Lieutenant Ysol Marrat, an Ossent officer raised in the uplands, offers the foundry a full order if her patrols get a distinct muster signal. The villages sent one pattern that both sides’ drovers already recognize. Their envoy can pay for one set today. Ossent conditions wider cooperation against Veyr on suspending Crownsunder’s throne claim. Neither delegation here can settle that demand. The drovers can still set up a local warning. Each side can still act through households.
Run a field test across a noisy quay. The officer places a drummer to simulate a troop moving. A drover listens from behind a warehouse. A bell that sounds distinct in a workshop can be confused at distance. Let V rearrange listeners, test different strokes, try flags by daylight or invite workers to a blind test. A hired wagon is due to carry the first set when the founder has a buyer. The officer can offer safer transport and much more metal, but military association may make a civilian bell a target. Private purchase preserves local control and can initially cover fewer hamlets.
Secure household bells, negotiate a separately recognizable patrol signal, place the order with the crown, or expose an unsafe design and stop the shipment. The officer can walk away and hire another founder. The first shipment or its cancellation is a complete city result. A later invitation to the Orseld uplands opens the field expedition below. A receipt from the villages takes real travel time.
The common beneath the white stones • Ossent’s lowland hinterland
At the remount yards, country carriers sell single muddy boots as a joke. A drover recently climbed a fence in one boot because someone moved the stones marking the common. Follow the road out, with a quoted travel time, to where arable strips meet shared pasture. A manor tenant moved the markers uphill to secure a bank that shelters her lambs. She knew she was taking common ground. Below it, giant boars have begun rooting through fields after a newly built estate fence cut their old path to the woods. The manor offers pay for a kill. The herdsmen offer guides and a seasonal grazing in a paddock under their control for the party’s animals if their route is restored.
The large boar's charges and damage to working fields establish the hunting danger; show tracks, breached fencing and a clear escape route. Hunting, defending workers or driving the animal off are available approaches; no live-capture term is assumed. NPC reactions follow their established values under RULES. Walk the ground before choosing sides. An old hedge ends at the true boundary. Hoofprints funnel into a sunken lane. A broken oak at its far end opens onto the woods. One boar is visible from an old hay loft, and ordinary sows with young are behind the bank. The villagers can supply hurdles. Open the oak end and drive the animals out, hunt the large boar from cover, or protect the fields while the parties reset the fence. Exposing the tenant can restore common grazing but costs a family its shelter unless someone finds other ground.
A successful hunt pays if accepted on those terms. A restored corridor protects fields over time, at the cost of part of the estate enclosure. A negotiated shelter strip may win grazing for both groups. If the party goes late, the manor has hired hunters and the next playable problem is getting penned stock and people clear of their drive. Resolving the immediate animal threat closes the agreed hunt. The pasture/boundary dispute can remain with the people concerned; V need not hold a hearing or complete another task to earn an agreed hunt payment.
The false fire on the ridge • Orseld Hills expedition
This is a separate trip into the Ossent–Crownsunder uplands. Set the route, supplies, days and rest category from the departure point before an offer is accepted. An invitation can come back from the bell job or independently through a shepherd returning for market. Two hill households once used a high fold for animals separated by bad weather. Now a group of armed stock thieves use its abandoned wall and a second, false signal fire to funnel herds into a blind valley. They sell on the Ossent side. The last animals taken included those pledged to a Crownsunder household. A local patrol would love to call the thefts enemy incursions.
At ground level a herder identifies a ewe by the swallowtail cut in her ear. On the ridge, the false beacon is visible from the fold yet hidden from the real watch shelter. At the wall, two narrow drove roads meet at a swing gate. A lookout has a horn. Two thieves sort stock below and a mounted buyer waits outside with a reserve pony. The lookout can see the gate from 50 feet up the slope. The buyer cannot. A horn buys him warning unless someone reaches the lookout first. Scouts can see all three positions before committing. Block the buyer’s route, turn the gate and recover the herd, lure the lookout off the high ground, or invite the patrol and risk an armed border incident. If the real watch has acquired bells, the villagers have an independent way to warn the next flock.
Recover marked animals while the buyer flees, capture the buyer to disrupt resale but lose part of the flock, or arrange a witnessed return at the fold without either patrol crossing the line. Bargaining for the gate or the buyer’s information is possible. On a late return the stock may already have been sold in Ossent. Follow the marks to an auction or prove the route to the patrol. Recovering the herd, disabling the false beacon and closing this outlet can finish the local operation without capturing the buyer. His flight does not erase a successful recovery; any further investigation is optional. Thieves who escape may seek another buyer elsewhere. The states’ succession dispute remains beyond this expedition.
