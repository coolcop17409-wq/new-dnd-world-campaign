The last passenger boat
Status and prerequisite
Later-return optional chapter; read FERRY SETUP and actual Chapter I outcome. Surveillance/rescue can succeed without a fight. Smuggling alone is not an immediate lethal threat.
Learn the route
From public steps 25 ft above, an observer sees the lamp operator but the canopy hides unloading hands. At the fenders, lighter/cases are visible while the lamp housing hides the signaler. Use the lower landing or a second observer to see the recipient; someone who actually knows that face can identify it.
A worker recognizes the patched sail and recalls three stops without passengers, not which future run carries lace. Compare separate accounts or agree on a signal; never assume active Whispers.
Coxswain's choice
She knows her own runs and admits some crew knowingly carried cases. She worries about passengers getting home. Clearing them before any seizure can earn the sailing time and her help; wages endangered or an unsafe transfer can change her judgment.
Hear her concern and V's actual offer. An uncertain promise uses DC14 Persuasion. Threats may obtain directions but cost voluntary help and prompt a crew warning. She can privately tell Desen her own part; others choose their testimony. Cirren promises only protection/truthful reporting within his power. Public fare stays available.
Transfer and possible conflict
On a genuine run, two 30-ft hulls lie alongside. The last case is on the lighter beside a 5-ft amidships plank; passengers/spare oars are 15 ft aft. The lace merchant waits at the lower steps and is distinct from the prospective ferry buyer. The pilot watches the deputy's lamp; the operator's enforcer moves the case.
V can mark cargo, follow, watch ashore or intervene. A warned coxswain asks customs to wait for passengers to clear. The deputy can warn only from information actually received in time. An aborted transfer leaves cargo aboard; witnessing an abort does not identify an unseen intended receiver.
The pilot values his boat over the case: normally he recalls the plank and uses an action to cast off the bow. Porters withdraw the plank after people clear or drop it/take cover under attack. Show everyone's hull and the shifting plank. With stern fast, bow swings; freeing the stern needs the pilot to move aft and act, or a willing hand there. A person at a line can secure or oppose it. Only actual rowing opens a 10-ft gap at round end.
Use 2014 Bandit Captain pilot/Thug enforcer if needed; danger to people, their actions and RULES govern force. Neither is scripted to fight to death.
Rescue and closure
The enforcer cannot swim and drops lace to grab a rail. The ferry has a buoyed rescue line; coxswain can turn the stern, and a willing passenger can free an oar. A skiff is downstream only if arranged. The enforcer witnessed a payment for inspection warning; if rescued, he may seek protection and offer that observation, not automatic immunity.
A sighted recipient, traced cargo, stopped transfer or safe passenger landing can finish this chapter. Evidence need not come from a captive. Refer the specific customs findings and return to ordinary life or Chapter III only if its independent trigger occurs.
